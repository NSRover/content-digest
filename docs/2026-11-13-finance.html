Commodity Markets Are Preparing for Something
gemini-flash (gemini-2.5-flash)fallback
TLDR

The recent surge in copper prices, often attributed to the AI boom, is now correcting despite persistent supply constraints. This suggests that demand expectations, particularly for AI-driven growth and global economic strength, may have been overly optimistic and are now being revised downwards. The weakening of copper, alongside other industrial metals and currencies like the Australian dollar, points to broader concerns about global liquidity and economic fragility rather than a robust, synchronized expansion.

Key points
  • Copper prices are correcting from record highs, despite strong supply-side factors like large inventories locked in the US due to tariff uncertainty and tight Chinese inventories with record import premiums. This indicates that demand concerns are now outweighing supply constraints.
  • The initial copper rally was partly driven by the anticipation of US tariffs on refined copper, leading to a massive redirection of metal to American warehouses, creating an artificial shortage outside the US, particularly in China.
  • Chinese economic data shows a disconnect between rising upstream production costs and weakening downstream consumer prices, indicating margin compression for fabricators and a lack of genuine demand-driven inflation.
  • While AI-related capital expenditures are increasing, there's limited evidence of a broader economic spillover in terms of employment, wages, or industrial production, suggesting that AI's profitability beyond a few tech giants is still uncertain.
  • Other industrial metals like silver, iron, and steel are not confirming a synchronized global boom, with silver struggling and iron/steel demand remaining weak, especially in China's property and heavy industry sectors.
  • The strengthening US dollar and weakening of commodity-linked currencies like the Australian dollar and Japanese yen reflect increasing risk aversion and demand for dollar liquidity, which are typically hostile conditions for commodity prices and global growth.
  • The continued fall in copper prices despite ongoing supply squeezes suggests that the AI boom may not be strong enough to overcome underlying weaknesses in global demand, Chinese economic fragility, and tightening dollar liquidity.
