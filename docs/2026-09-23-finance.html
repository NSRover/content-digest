You Won’t Believe What the Bond Market Just Did

TLDR

The Treasury yield curve is flattening toward inversion, with the 2-year and 10-year spread narrowing to 20 basis points because long-term yields are falling rather than short-term yields rising. While the front end of the curve prices in the Federal Reserve's short-term rate hikes and energy-driven inflation fears, the long end is beginning to price in the resulting demand destruction and credit tightening. This divergence suggests that the market is preparing for the economic consequences of the Fed's actions, which could ultimately force interest rates lower.

Key points
  • The spread between the 2-year and 10-year Treasury yields has flattened to 20 basis points, bringing the yield curve close to inversion. Unlike previous flattening driven by rising short-term yields, this recent shift occurred because the 10-year yield fell while the 2-year yield remained relatively stable.
  • The front end of the curve reflects expectations of Federal Reserve rate hikes in response to rising oil and diesel prices. However, the long end of the curve is pricing in the demand destruction caused by these higher energy costs, which act as a tax on businesses and consumers and ultimately weaken the real economy.
  • Despite fears of an energy shock, demand for inflation protection in Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) remains benign. This indicates that investors do not expect the current oil price spike to transition into a lasting, self-sustaining inflationary regime.
  • Corporate bond market behavior reveals a shared expectation of falling rates, as companies are avoiding issuing long-term debt to prevent locking in high rates, while investors are aggressively buying the limited supply of 30-year bonds to lock in today's yields.
  • Signs of credit tightening are emerging at the margins of the financial system, with triple C-rated corporate credit spreads widening toward 1100 basis points and financing for major AI infrastructure projects becoming more expensive and difficult to distribute.

A $40 Trillion Currency Reset Just Started.

TLDR

The US Treasury is implementing a "Treasury twist" strategy to manage the national debt by shifting from long-term to short-term bonds, with stablecoins becoming a major buyer of this short-term debt. This plan aims to allow the government to continue spending at low interest rates, but its success hinges on the Federal Reserve maintaining low rates, which may lead to conflict given the Fed's current rate hikes.

Key points
  • The US Treasury, under Scott Bessant, is actively intervening in the bond market through buybacks of long-term bonds and increasing the size of short-term bond auctions. This is a strategic move to reduce reliance on long-term debt, where foreign investors dictate interest rates, and shift towards short-term debt, where the Federal Reserve controls the rates.
  • The US government faces a significant debt problem, with obligations exceeding tax revenue, and this gap is projected to widen. Traditional solutions like raising taxes or cutting spending are not being pursued, leading to the need for unconventional strategies to manage borrowing costs.
  • Geopolitical risks, such as the US freezing assets of other nations and using the financial system as a weapon, are causing foreign institutional buyers to demand higher interest rates for US long-term debt or pull back from buying altogether, eroding trust in the US financial system.
  • The "Genius Act" of July 2025, initially perceived as crypto-focused, mandates that US dollar stablecoins be 100% backed by liquid assets, primarily short-term treasuries. This has created a direct link between the stablecoin market and the US government bond market.
  • Stablecoins have rapidly become a significant buyer of short-term US government bonds, projected to become the largest holder of US debt by 2030. This demand is driven by individuals in emerging markets seeking a stable store of value against their volatile local currencies, often without realizing they are funding US government debt.
  • The success of the "Treasury twist" depends on the Federal Reserve keeping interest rates low. However, the Fed is currently raising rates, creating a potential conflict between the government's debt management strategy and the Fed's monetary policy, which could impact the value of the dollar.

The Energy Crisis Is About to Become a Debt Crisis | Peter Carlin.
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

The global energy market is facing a severe structural crisis driven by soaring diesel refining margins, tight supplies of sour crude, and a lack of natural gas storage in Europe. This energy shock is directly feeding into inflation, rising government debt, and a looming sovereign-debt crisis across G7 nations. As physical resources and energy move to the highest bidders in Asia, gold remains a critical asset for preserving wealth amid a broader crisis of confidence.

Key points
  • The global diesel market is in a severe crisis, with refining margins (crack spreads) reaching $100 compared to a historical norm of $10 to $15. This is driven by the loss of refining capacity in the Middle East and green energy policies in Europe, which led the EU to halve its diesel refining capacity over the past 15 years.
  • The United States Gulf Coast refining system is currently upholding the entire world's diesel supply, exporting 1.9 million barrels a day. However, US refineries have been running at an unsustainable 97% to 98% capacity for weeks, raising the risk of unscheduled outages due to delayed maintenance.
  • Europe is highly vulnerable to natural gas shortages, particularly the UK, which holds only about five days of gas storage and relies entirely on continuous pipeline throughput from Norway and the North Sea. European natural gas prices have risen up to 20 times what they should be, with UK and EU prices up 170% and 185% year-to-date respectively.
  • Global competition for energy is intensifying as Asian buyers, particularly China, outbid Europe for liquefied natural gas (LNG) and sour crude. Shanghai crude oil has traded at a $25 per barrel premium over the Brent system, illustrating how physical resources are flowing to the highest bidder.
  • The energy crisis is accelerating a sovereign-debt crisis in the West, as heavily indebted governments face the choice of letting energy suppliers go bankrupt or bailing them out with taxpayer funds. This fiscal strain is pushing bond yields higher, with the UK 10-year yield at 5.25% and the French 10-year yield rising above Italy's.
  • In this fragile macroeconomic environment, gold serves as a critical hedge against a systemic crisis of confidence. Physical gold continues to migrate from West to East, supported by strong central bank buying, such as the People's Bank of China doubling its officially reported gold acquisitions.
