  • The current situation in 2026 mirrors these past instances. The rise in the 30-year bond yield is attributed to "Fed risk," with the market anticipating potential policy missteps. Signals like flattening yield curves (where short-term rates rise more than long-term rates), low and compressing swap spreads, and a lack of inflation risk priced into TIPS suggest that the market does not believe interest rates will continue to rise significantly or stay high.
