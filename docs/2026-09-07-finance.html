The Market’s Reaction to This Blowout Jobs Report Makes NO Sense
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

The bond market showed a notable lack of reaction to the seemingly strong August payroll report, signaling that investors are looking past the headline numbers. This muted response is driven by a long-running pattern of massive downward revisions to initially strong Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) data. Underlying labor indicators, such as flat average hourly earnings and dismal hiring rates, confirm that the broader economic trend remains weak.

Key points
  • The historical significance of "Payroll Friday" has severely diminished because the market now views initial BLS payroll numbers as highly unreliable. Over the past two years, initially high headline figures have consistently been revised downward months later, rendering the initial "blowout" reports virtually meaningless.
  • The BLS methodology is structurally ill-equipped for a slow-growth economy, as its statistical models assume either a standard post-war expansion or an active recession. This binary assumption, combined with the birth-death model, leads to consistent overstatements of job growth that must be corrected later.
  • Underlying labor market metrics do not support the narrative of a booming job market. Average hourly earnings for production and non-supervisory workers have remained flat, average weekly hours worked showed almost no increase, and the JOLTS hiring rate remains near its worst level of the current economic cycle.
  • Other macroeconomic indicators point to a cooling trend rather than a robust recovery. While manufacturing inventory has built up due to inflation fears, new order books are rolling over, and the services sector is experiencing a slight contraction in its employment subsector.
  • A K-shaped reality exists for businesses, where small and medium-sized enterprises are struggling with squeezed profit margins and stagnant revenues. Unlike large corporations, these smaller businesses stopped hiring years ago and are running barebones operations, which explains the lack of overall hiring without a massive wave of layoffs.
  • Global labor data, such as Canada's unexpected loss of full-time jobs in August, corroborates the downward trend of the economic mini-cycle. Across multiple countries, falling labor force participation rates mask the true extent of unemployment, as discouraged workers drop out of the labor force entirely when hiring stalls.

What You need to Understand about the BRICS Secret Plan to replace U.S. Dollar Dominance
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Despite persistent media headlines predicting the death of the U.S. dollar, the BRICS nations have failed to implement any cohesive, material changes to challenge Western financial dominance. Over 17 years of summits, the group has established only one functioning institution, which itself operates primarily in U.S. dollars and relies on the Western financial system. Prominent BRICS leaders and central bankers have repeatedly stated on the record that they are not pursuing de-dollarization.

Key points
  • The BRICS organization is characterized by repetitive rhetoric rather than action, with its annual summit communiqués remaining virtually identical year after year. In the core paragraphs of the 2024 and 2025 declarations, 89 out of 91 words were completely identical, with only a numeral and a comma being changed.
  • Out of sixteen announced initiatives since 2009, the New Development Bank (NDB) is the only working institution that has actually been built and operated. The other signed treaty, the Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA), has never been used and remains dependent on the IMF for 70% of its funding access.
  • The NDB is a small-scale player that operates as a U.S. dollar-based institution rather than a dollar competitor. Over nine years, the NDB disbursed only $24 billion—less than half of what the Western-backed International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) committed in fiscal year 2025 alone—and roughly 65% of its loans are approved in U.S. dollars.
  • BRICS officials and member nations openly reject the narrative of de-dollarization. Leaders and central bankers from India, Russia, South Africa, and Brazil have explicitly stated that they are not seeking to replace the dollar, with Vladimir Putin recently expressing support for restoring full-scale relations with the U.S. dollar system.
  • The narrative of a "BRICS currency" is the latest in a long line of failed "dollar killers" promoted by commentators since 1999. Previous hyped alternatives—including the Euro, the Shanghai Gold Exchange, CIPS, the petroyuan, INSTEX, Libra/Diem, the e-yuan, InBridge, and "the unit"—have all failed to displace the U.S. dollar's global dominance.
