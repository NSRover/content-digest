Verging on Another Escalation in the Middle East | Ali Vaez

TLDR

The war between Iran and the United States, eight months into Operation Epic Fury, has escalated into a regional confrontation involving Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and a stalemate between Iran and the US. Iran has effectively used its control over the Strait of Hormuz as a tool for economic warfare, impacting global energy prices and depleting US military resources. The conflict has severely impacted Iran's economy and society, while also leading to a breakdown in US-Israel coordination and a re-evaluation of military strategies by both sides.

Key points
  • The conflict has metastasized into a wider regional confrontation, with Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia gaining an independent dynamic from the direct US-Iran conflict. This complicates Washington's position, forcing a choice between upholding a ceasefire with the Houthis and maintaining credibility with Gulf allies.
  • Iran has effectively leveraged its geographic control of the Strait of Hormuz to exert pressure on the global economy, causing significant increases in energy prices and insurance premiums. This represents a new form of economic warfare where a country under sanctions can retaliate by impacting global markets.
  • The war has severely depleted US missile interceptor stockpiles (e.g., 70% of THAAD, 50% of Patriot missiles), raising concerns about short-term preparedness for other geopolitical black swans. It has also led to the targeting and effective expulsion of US military bases from the region, forcing a strategic withdrawal to Jordan and Israel.
  • The conflict has had a profound impact on Iran's internal dynamics, shifting public anger from solely the regime to a shared animosity towards the US and Israel. Economically, Iran faces hyperinflation, increased unemployment, and a significant currency devaluation, yet the regime has been energized and its core constituents mobilized.
  • Coordination between the US and Israel has diminished, partly due to Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu's overestimation of the ease of conflict with Iran and the high costs Israel incurred from Iranian targeting. This has led to a deficit of trust in Israeli assessments by the US.
  • Russia and China are benefiting from the conflict by bogging down the US military with minimal investment, providing intelligence and military support to Iran. This low-cost proxy strategy allows them to deplete US resources that could otherwise be used in other theaters like Ukraine or Taiwan.
  • The war has prompted a re-evaluation of military strategies, with the US potentially learning to fight in more asymmetric ways, similar to Iran, by developing cheaper technologies to counter inexpensive drones, rather than using costly interceptors.
