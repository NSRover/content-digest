Why rising Rates are a Problem for the USA & a Death Sentence for Short Stacks at the Poker table.
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Rising global interest rates and a strengthening US dollar are creating a severe "dollar squeeze" that disproportionately harms foreign economies compared to the United States. While higher rates pressure the US, its status as the global hegemon with the unique ability to print the world's primary reserve currency allows it to withstand the pressure far better than "short-stacked" foreign nations. Consequently, capital is fleeing vulnerable international markets and seeking refuge in US equities and gold.

Key points
  • The global financial system resembles a poker game where rising interest rates act as rising blinds that force players to act. The US is the "big stack" at the table because it can print the chips (dollars) required to play, whereas other countries are "short stacks" highly vulnerable to being blinded out.
  • China does not participate at the main international table because its bond market is over 95% domestically owned, closed to foreign capital, and restricted by currency controls. Its falling interest rates are driven by domestic deflation and a real estate collapse rather than global market dynamics.
  • Foreign nations face a double burden because they must manage two distinct carry trades: their domestic currency operations and their offshore dollar-denominated operations. When the dollar strengthens alongside rising global rates, both carry trades become highly difficult to sustain simultaneously.
  • The Bank for International Settlements estimates that off-balance sheet dollar debt outside the US—including currency swaps and forwards—exceeds $100 trillion. During a currency crisis, foreign central banks are forced to act as guarantors of this massive debt, rapidly depleting their resources.
  • Over the last eight years, major currencies like the yen and the real have depreciated by 45% and over 60% respectively against the dollar, while global bond prices have plummeted. This currency devaluation has driven international investors and foreign central banks to aggressively buy gold as a refuge.
