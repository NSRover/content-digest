WTF Just Happened to Diesel...
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

While the national average retail price for diesel has already reached a record of approximately $6.20 per gallon, geopolitical disruptions and refining bottlenecks mean that $8, $9, or even $10 diesel is a real risk. Because diesel is the primary fuel of the industrial economy, such extreme price spikes would redirect consumer income away from discretionary spending, potentially triggering severe demand destruction and job losses. Furthermore, these rising energy costs are prompting the bond market to price in additional Federal Reserve rate hikes, increasing the risk of the central bank overreacting and pushing a vulnerable economy into an outright contraction.

Key points
  • The current diesel crisis is driven by a global shortage of finished fuel rather than just expensive crude oil, as refining capacity has become a major bottleneck. Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refineries, a Russian diesel export ban, and damaged refining capacity and shipping disruptions in the Middle East have severely limited global fuel distribution.
  • Refineries are physically constrained and cannot easily shift production to meet the diesel shortage without consequences. Producing more diesel and heating oil means producing less gasoline, which would tighten gasoline supplies and transfer price pressures directly to everyday drivers.
  • Proposed policy interventions, such as a domestic diesel export ban, carry significant trade-offs. Energy companies warn that restricting exports could reduce the incentive to refine fuel in the U.S., disrupt established trade flows, and actually raise domestic gasoline prices by 15 to 30 cents per gallon or more.
  • Rising energy prices have reconnected the two-year U.S. Treasury yield to oil prices, signaling that the bond market expects the Federal Reserve to react to temporary energy inflation by raising policy rates. However, higher interest rates cannot fix physical refining shortages, and further hikes risk overtightening into an economy already experiencing demand destruction.
  • Historical precedents, such as the 1990–1991 recession triggered by the energy price spike after Iraq's invasion of Kuwait, show that energy shocks do not lead to endless inflation. Instead, they temporarily raise consumer prices while ultimately destroying demand, weakening employment, and pushing vulnerable economies into recession.

The Bond Market Is Breaking — And Gold Could Be Heading to $8,000

TLDR

A long-term logarithmic chart analysis suggests that gold could realistically reach $8,000 by the end of 2027, which could drive silver to a minimum of $250 to $260 if the gold-to-silver ratio drops to 30. This potential surge is supported by a major trend reversal in the bond market, where a 40-year bull market has ended and rising Treasury yields are threatening the global debt bubble. Central banks remain trapped between a rock and a hard place, as raising interest rates aggressively to combat cost-push inflation risks triggering severe recessions and expanding government deficits.

Key points
  • A monthly logarithmic chart for gold indicates a highly bullish long-term trend, with a parabolic curve pointing toward a potential price of $8,000 by the end of 2027.
  • If gold reaches $8,000 and the gold-to-silver ratio drops to 30—matching its 2011 low—silver would trade at approximately $266, with a potential move to a ratio of 20 pushing silver to $400.
  • The 40-year bull market in government bonds has ended, with both 10-year and 30-year U.S. Treasury yields breaking out of long-term downward trends and signaling much higher yields ahead.
  • Central banks are trapped by gargantuan global debt; aggressive rate hikes to curb cost-push inflation from the energy crisis risk halting the economy, reducing tax revenues, and forcing a return to money printing.
  • Historical patterns suggest that sovereign debt and currency crises often occur around the autumn equinox, meaning the market could experience significant volatility and drying liquidity starting in late September 2026.
  • While rising yields may cause a correction in the overvalued general stock market and short-term turbulence for mining stocks, the medium- to long-term outlook for gold and silver miners remains highly bullish.
