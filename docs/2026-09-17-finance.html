LIVE: Will the Fed Really Raise Rates Into a Struggling Economy
gemini-flash (gemini-2.5-flash)fallback
TLDR

The Federal Reserve hiked interest rates for symbolic reasons, aiming to project seriousness about inflation despite economic data, bond market signals, and consumer sentiment suggesting a struggling economy and fragile labor market. This divergence indicates that the rate hiking cycle is unlikely to be prolonged, as the underlying economic conditions do not justify sustained increases.

Key points
  • The Federal Reserve's decision to hike rates is primarily symbolic, intended to convey a strong stance against inflation, rather than being justified by robust economic data.
  • The Fed views the labor market as resilient based on the official unemployment rate, but this ignores declining labor force participation and rising unofficial unemployment, which consumers attribute to a lack of jobs and recession-like conditions.
  • Consumer sentiment surveys, such as the University of Michigan survey, show historically low confidence and widespread fear of unemployment, consistent with recessionary periods, directly contradicting the Fed's assessment of a resilient economy.
  • While the Fed points to the PCE deflator as a reason for concern about inflation, a closer look at the trimmed mean PCE deflator reveals that underlying inflation trends are improving, and the headline PCE deflator may be overstated and subject to downward revision.
  • The bond market and American consumers largely disagree with the Fed's inflation narrative, viewing rising oil prices in a fragile labor market as leading to demand destruction, not inflation, which is reflected in the flat yield curve.
  • The Fed's reliance on "expectations theory" to justify rate hikes is criticized as unsubstantiated and a substitute for genuine monetary knowledge, suggesting that their policy is more about communication and sentiment manipulation than effective economic management.
  • Despite the rate hike, the bond market showed little immediate reaction, and the Fed's own projections do not suggest a prolonged or aggressive hiking cycle, implying that any further increases would also be largely symbolic.

An Opportunity Like This Won’t Come Again (Emergency Update)
gemini-flash (gemini-2.5-flash)fallback
TLDR

The current AI investment boom is one of the largest and fastest in modern economic history, exceeding past bubbles like the dot-com bust and the roaring twenties. While the economy is currently strengthening, historical patterns suggest that overinvestment in new technologies eventually leads to significant market downturns and decades of financial pain. Investors should be prepared for a potential shift in the business cycle around June 2027, especially with the Federal Reserve raising interest rates.

Key points
  • Major technological revolutions, while transformative, consistently lead to investment booms that end in severe market corrections and prolonged financial pain, not shallow pullbacks. Examples include the dot-com bust, the Great Depression following the roaring twenties, and Britain's railway mania.
  • A key indicator of an impending bust is "overinvestment," where companies build infrastructure for a new technology far ahead of actual consumer demand, as seen with electricity in the 1920s and fiber optics during the dot-com era.
  • A warning sign of overinvestment is when the companies driving the boom (like today's AI hyperscalers) begin to underperform the broader market, indicating investors are pulling funding. This pattern has historically preceded broader market declines by about six months.
  • The business cycle, amplified by technological booms, is a critical factor. The ISM Purchasing Managers Index (PMI) indicates current economic strength, and while it's currently accelerating, a peak and decline in PMI have historically signaled market downturns.
  • The yield curve, a leading indicator, suggests the current economic expansion could continue until June 2027. However, its recent rollover indicates tightening liquidity, which, combined with Federal Reserve interest rate hikes, could mark a significant pivot point for the market and the AI investment boom.

The Market Is Mispricing A Correlation Shock | Dean Curnutt
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Unusually low stock correlation within the S&P 500 is suppressing index-level realized volatility, creating a massive spread between single-stock and index volatility. This dynamic has fueled a crowded "dispersion trade" and pushed implied correlation to levels that offer no margin of safety. Given the combination of fiscal deficits, Treasury market stress, and potential alignment among AI stocks, tail hedging via S&P 500 volatility and VIX options presents a compelling opportunity.

Key points
  • The realized correlation among S&P 500 stocks is trending at historically low levels of 5% to 15%, compared to a typical benign market average of 35% to 40%. This lack of correlation acts as a diversification windfall that artificially dampens index-level volatility.
  • The dispersion trade, heavily utilized by quantitative investment strategies (QIS) and pod shops, involves selling index correlation to capture carry. Because this trade has been highly successful, it has pushed implied correlation down to extreme lows, leaving the market vulnerable to a sudden correlation shock.
  • Traditional positive-carry hedges, such as long positions in Treasury bonds, have broken down because the bond market itself has become a primary source of risk to the equity market. With the U.S. adding $860 billion in debt over just four months during peace time, the 10-year note is behaving as a risk asset.
  • The AI trade has created a false sense of diversification, as hyperscalers and chipmakers currently trade with very low correlation. However, these top-heavy S&P 500 components are highly susceptible to a joint downward correlation event if the massive capital expenditure cycle in AI begins to slow.
  • Investors should consider systematic tail hedging using VIX calls or call spreads rather than trying to time the market. Because low realized correlation is holding down implied volatility, financial insurance is priced attractively relative to the prevailing macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainties.
