TLDR

Mainstream economic analysis relies heavily on backward-looking data and Federal Reserve narratives, whereas observing ledger-based eurodollar system signals provides a clearer forward-looking picture of macroeconomic regime changes. Current market signals—including flat yield curves, negative swap spreads, low TIPS inflation expectations, and widening Triple-C corporate junk bond spreads—indicate growing defensive and deflationary pressures rather than a sustained inflationary expansion. Because major credit and economic downturns are slow-moving processes, tracking structural interest rate curves and credit thresholds allows investors to identify real pivot points well before they impact public markets.

Key points
  • Mainstream economics lost track of the global monetary system when money shifted to offshore, ledger-based eurodollars, forcing central banks to rely on backward-looking econometric models instead of forward-looking monetary data.
  • Recent price action in indicators like the copper-to-gold ratio and spikes in long-term Treasury yields do not signal true economic reflation, as copper supply is distorted by US tariff threats and yield spikes are driven by illiquidity, repo market inelasticity, and seasonal calendar effects.
  • Defensive market behavior is evident across global derivative markets, where negative interest rate swap spreads indicate dealer balance sheet constraints and a lack of offensive risk-taking.
  • Triple-C junk bond spreads reaching 1,300 basis points serve as a critical credit cycle warning point; a further widening toward 1,500 basis points and expansion into higher-grade corporate credit would confirm a broader credit cycle breakdown.
  • Credit stress is actively spreading into private credit, levered loans, BDCs, and AI debt, creating systemic vulnerability for equity markets that heavily depend on debt market financing.
  • Real macroeconomic regime shifts develop over multi-year periods, giving investors time to interpret market curves and credit signals rather than reacting to short-term Federal Reserve commentary or media noise.
