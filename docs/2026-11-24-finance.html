TLDR

Cognitive scientist Tania Lombrozo explores how humans construct explanations, gain true understanding, and fall victim to cognitive illusions. The conversation details how jargon and overly simplistic models trick people into a false sense of comprehension, while distinguishing predictive accuracy from genuine mental mapping. It also examines how individuals can evaluate expert credibility without domain expertise, navigate the limits of scientific truth, and address declining public trust in institutions.

Key points
  • Cognitive science combines empirical tools from psychology with conceptual frameworks from philosophy to study how everyday explanations drive learning, shape beliefs, and dictate decision-making.
  • Explaining differs from accumulating isolated facts; true understanding requires an integrated mental model that highlights dependencies, allowing an individual to generalize knowledge to unfamiliar scenarios rather than relying on predictive accuracy or rote memorization.
  • Humans are highly susceptible to an "illusion of understanding," often finding poor explanations satisfying simply because they contain technical jargon (such as fabricated terms), which the mind assumes acts as a placeholder for expert knowledge.
  • Non-experts can assess the credibility of sources—including artificial intelligence models—by identifying internal logical inconsistencies, uncovering potential conflicts of interest, and evaluating the underlying methodologies used to arrive at claims.
  • Human understanding typically relies on multiple piecemeal mental models built for specific contexts rather than a unified theory of reality, making metacognition—the ability to choose the appropriate model for a given problem—a vital skill.
  • Epistemic commitments are broader than binary true-or-false choices; expanding psychological and philosophical language allows individuals to accept claims provisionally, adopt working assumptions, or utilize scientific frameworks purely for their practical utility.
