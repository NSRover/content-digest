The New Science Of Female Attraction - Psychology Roundtable

Guest

Macken Murphy is an evolutionary biologist at the University of Melbourne, a writer, and a podcaster. Steve Stewart-Williams is an evolutionary psychologist, professor, and author. Rob Henderson holds a PhD in psychology from the University of Cambridge and is a Senior Fellow at the Manhattan Institute.

TLDR

This roundtable explores the evolutionary psychology of female attraction, challenging common myths about dating and sex differences. It delves into how societal changes, economic factors, and individual psychology shape mate preferences and behaviors, highlighting the persistent, yet sometimes nuanced, differences between men and women in what they seek and how they attract partners.

Key points
  • The "ick" in dating, particularly for women, may be an evolved mechanism to detect deception or negative personality traits like insecurity, rather than just a reaction to artificiality. Women's higher choosiness makes them more prone to this reaction.
  • The idea that a small percentage of "Chad" men monopolize all women is largely an illusion created by dating app algorithms and social media, which amplify the experiences of a small, highly promiscuous group of women, rather than reflecting the reality of most people's sexual lives.
  • Economic conditions significantly influence mate preferences; both men and women tend to prioritize resources more when they or their sex are economically disadvantaged. This suggests a strategic adjustment of preferences based on environmental incentives.
  • Male beautification and increased parenting effort are growing trends, potentially driven by a response to reduced socioeconomic imbalances between men and women, leading men to compete on other domains of attractiveness.
  • Minimizing or denying inherent sex differences can have detrimental consequences, leading to misunderstandings in relationships, unrealistic expectations, and a failure to address sex-specific health and psychological needs.

Guest

Michael Saylor is the founder and Executive Chairman of Strategy, formerly MicroStrategy, which was the first publicly traded company to adopt Bitcoin as its primary treasury asset. He holds dual degrees from MIT in aerospace engineering and the history of science, is a named inventor on more than 48 patents, and authored "The Mobile Wave." His company holds 847,000 Bitcoin, making him one of the largest holders globally.

TLDR

The dollar consistently loses value, making traditional savings and even real estate less effective for wealth preservation. Bitcoin is presented as a superior long-term capital asset due to its historical returns and resistance to debasement, offering digital empowerment. AI is a transformative tool for generating novel business ideas and solutions, and individuals should learn to leverage it to create new value and navigate a future where basic needs might be met by automation, but scarce goods and experiences will still drive demand for money.

Key points
  • The US dollar has consistently lost approximately 7% of its value annually for the past century, quietly eroding the purchasing power of savings and making traditional assets like cash in a bank a losing strategy.
  • Bitcoin is presented as the best long-term capital asset, offering digital empowerment and superior historical returns compared to gold, the S&P 500, or diversified tech stocks, making it a compelling option for individuals seeking to preserve and grow wealth over the long term.
  • While real estate can preserve wealth, it comes with significant tax and maintenance burdens, and commercial real estate is generally a better option than residential due to potential rental income offsetting expenses.
  • AI is a powerful tool for innovation, capable of generating novel business ideas and financial instruments that have never existed before, and individuals should become adept at using AI to ask it to do new things rather than just learning existing tasks.
  • The future economy, driven by AI and robotics, may lead to an age of abundance where basic needs are met, but money will still be valuable for scarce, desirable goods and experiences that AI cannot infinitely create, maintaining a hierarchy of affluence.
  • To thrive in an AI-driven world, individuals should focus their energy, guard their time, train their minds and bodies, think for themselves, curate their friends and environment, keep promises, stay cheerful and constructive, and aim to "upgrade the world" by leveraging new technologies.
  • Long-term thinking and building foundational decisions that create compounding competitive advantages are crucial for success, as is identifying "magic opportunities" at the right point on a technology's S-curve where it becomes commercially viable.
