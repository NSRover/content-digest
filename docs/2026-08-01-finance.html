TLDR

The recent correction in AI stocks is not a sign of a bursting bubble, but rather a healthy valuation correction concentrated in the AI built-out layer. Major hyperscalers like Meta, Microsoft, and Google are continuing to increase their capital expenditure on AI infrastructure, indicating that the long-term AI theme remains intact. Retail investors should view this volatility as an opportunity to aggregate high-quality AI stocks at sensible valuations rather than panicking.

Key points
  • The AI market is segmented into three distinct layers: hyperscalers (like Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, and Google), AI built-out companies (like Micron and Oracle providing memory and compute power), and consumer-facing applications. The recent market correction is primarily affecting the second layer, the AI built-out space, which is undergoing a valuation correction after massive multi-bagger runs.
  • Claims that the AI bubble has burst due to corrections in indices like South Korea's Kospi or individual stocks like Micron are misleading. Even after a 40% correction, stocks like Micron remain up significantly over a multi-year period, and hyperscalers like Nvidia are still up over 150% over the last two years despite recent drawdowns.
  • Financial results from major tech companies show that the fundamental business cases for AI remain strong. Companies like Meta, Microsoft, and Google are reporting growing revenues and operating profits that double roughly every five years, and they are actively committing to increasing their capital expenditures on data centers and compute capacity through 2027.
  • Active portfolio management and profit-booking are essential when investing in high-beta global stocks. Relying solely on a "buy and hold" strategy during massive run-ups can expose investors to unnecessary drawdowns, whereas rotating capital and booking profits allows investors to buy back into high-quality assets during market consolidations.
