Key points
  • The US bond market is showing signs of breaking, with borrowing costs for 2-year, 10-year, and 30-year Treasury bonds at their highest levels since major past crises.
  • This situation is driven by a competition for capital between the US government, whose debt has surged to $40 trillion and is accelerating, and major tech companies borrowing heavily for AI infrastructure.
  • Both the US government and big tech are in a critical need for capital, creating a high-stakes bidding war for limited investor funds, as acknowledged by Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell.
  • The potential for a "debt death spiral" exists if interest rates continue to rise, where rising interest payments consume the budget, erode investor confidence, and lead to a vicious cycle of increasing debt.
  • Governments typically have four options to address such a crisis: austerity (unlikely due to political challenges), raising taxes (with diminishing returns), default (unthinkable for the US), or currency debasement.
  • Currency debasement, achieved by expanding the money supply, is presented as the most probable solution, where the government artificially suppresses interest rates by printing money to buy bonds, thereby reducing the real burden of debt.
  • This debasement strategy, historically used, can lead to inflation and a loss of purchasing power for the currency, potentially causing asset prices like stocks to rise as cash loses value, while those holding cash see their savings diminish.
