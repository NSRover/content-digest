The 10-Minute Portfolio Review Every Investor Should Do! (A step by step process)
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Reviewing and balancing an investment portfolio is critical to avoid zero or negative returns, especially when accounting for currency depreciation. Investors should track their holdings on a single spreadsheet, identify a relevant benchmark, calculate their portfolio's beta, and minimize wealth-destroying commissions. Additionally, managing risk through hedging strategies, asset correlation, and a clear core investing thesis is essential for long-term portfolio health.

Key points
  • Investors must consolidate all their holdings from various accounts into a single Excel spreadsheet to track percentage allocations. For Indian equities, this can be done using the Consolidated Account Statement (CAS), while US investments can be tracked via platforms like Vested.
  • Every portfolio needs a relevant benchmark, such as the Nifty50 for Indian large caps or the QQQ for US tech, to accurately measure performance. Calculating the portfolio's beta relative to this benchmark helps investors understand its risk profile and sensitivity to market movements.
  • High commissions, such as those charged by certain mutual funds or Portfolio Management Services (PMS) schemes, can cause massive long-term wealth destruction. Investors should analyze their fees and consider direct stock investing to minimize these costs.
  • Portfolio risk should be mitigated using hedging strategies, such as maintaining a cash-to-investment ratio for downward averaging, buying put options on the benchmark index, or holding uncorrelated assets like gold, bonds, or real estate.
  • To protect against tail risk—where a single market or economic shock badly hits the entire portfolio—investors should diversify globally and avoid over-leveraging or borrowing money to buy individual stocks.
  • A successful portfolio must be guided by a mathematically driven core investing thesis rather than vague ideas of compounding. For example, a thesis centered on US tech dominance requires aligning the portfolio with global assets, as similar tech-dominant companies are not available in the Indian market.
