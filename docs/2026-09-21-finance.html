Will Trump Preside over the Wedding between the Fed & Treasury? And what would it mean for Markets?
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

The Federal Reserve and the US Treasury are separate institutions operating around the same asset—the US dollar—but their operational boundaries are increasingly blurring. This structural overlap, combined with shifting global dynamics away from a rules-based order toward an "America First" policy, is driving the two institutions toward an eventual, necessary "marriage." Because the Fed's independence is granted by Congress rather than the Constitution, its autonomy can ultimately be renegotiated or reclaimed by political authorities.

Key points
  • The Federal Reserve and the US Treasury perform distinct but highly overlapping roles centered on the US dollar. The Treasury manages government spending, debt issuance, and sanctions, while the Fed sets monetary policy, manages bank reserves, and acts as the lender of last resort.
  • Despite widespread talk of global de-dollarization, data shows that foreign holdings of US Treasuries have reached an all-time high, with the rest of the world purchasing $3.5 trillion in Treasuries over the last eight years.
  • The financial plumbing of both institutions is deeply circular; the Fed's assets consist primarily of Treasury paper, and the interest paid by the Treasury on those bonds is ultimately remitted back to the Treasury. Consolidating their balance sheets would cause a massive block of outstanding US debt to simply disappear.
  • The Treasury is increasingly encroaching on areas historically managed by the Fed, such as establishing swap lines with foreign nations like Argentina, restricting dollar access to Iran, and executing its own bond buyback programs.
  • Fed independence is operational and established by precedent—specifically the Fed-Treasury Accord of 1951—rather than constitutional law. This means Congress has the authority to alter or reclaim the Fed's independent status if political pressures demand it.
