America’s Housing Market Just Took a Dark Turn
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Existing home sales fell to a 16-year low, reflecting broader consumer anxiety and deep mistrust in the labor market despite official claims of economic resilience. The overall economy has become increasingly fragile, relying almost entirely on an AI-driven stock market wealth effect that has failed to generate widespread job growth or broader economic benefits. Consumers are reacting to this precarious environment by incrementally cutting spending, avoiding major financial commitments, and preparing for rising unemployment.

Key points
  • Existing home sales crashed to a 16-year low, and pending sales dropped to record lows, driven by falling consumer confidence and weakening paycheck growth rather than just high mortgage rates.
  • Rising mortgage delinquencies indicate that current homeowners are already struggling to keep up with housing costs and inflation, signaling deeper stress in the household sector.
  • Data from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York shows consumers are far more worried about stagnant hiring and rising unemployment—expecting joblessness to increase at rates not seen since 2020—than they are about sustained broad inflation.
  • Consumer spending cuts are happening incrementally at the margins rather than through a sudden cliff-like collapse, as households quietly reduce small purchases, avoid big-ticket items, and delay home buys.
  • The economy has grown dangerously fragile because its perceived strength is heavily concentrated in a narrow AI stock bubble, which provides a wealth effect for high earners but offers no plan B for the broader labor market if equity values or tech capital expenditures slow down.

  • Sovereign debt and rising bond yields represent the main threat to the global financial system. The US federal budget deficit is running extremely high at around 6% of GDP, which prevents true economic normalization and threatens market stability.

  • Copper presents a bullish long-term outlook due to massive projected global infrastructure demand over the next 15 to 20 years. However, gold and silver remain the primary tools for wealth preservation.

  • A closing liquidity valve is present, as the Federal Reserve has begun raising interest rates again with expectations for additional increases toward the end of the year.
  • Despite rate hikes, the National Financial Conditions Index remains below zero, confirming that financial conditions are still relatively soft and the system retains enough liquidity to prevent an immediate macro peak.
  • In the short term, the Nasdaq 100 has broken all key moving averages, signaling market volatility that could last for months due to underlying weakness in corporate bond markets.
