The “Safest” Companies Are Warning the Consumer Is Breaking
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

While the Federal Reserve and mainstream media warn of inflation risks due to rising oil prices and a "resilient" economy, the broader bond and commodities markets are signaling the exact opposite. Corporate reports from consumer-facing giants like Nestle reveal that raising prices is leading to volume contraction and demand destruction rather than sustained inflation. The overwhelming consensus across swaps, TIPS, and long-term oil curves is that the economy is not resilient, and the Fed's potential rate hikes will ultimately trigger economic contraction and subsequent rate cuts.

Key points
  • The Federal Reserve and central banks are focusing on inflation risks, pointing to rising oil prices and a supposedly resilient labor market to justify potential rate hikes. However, the broader marketplace—including the bond market, interest rate swaps, and TIPS—is looking in the opposite direction, pricing in demand destruction rather than sustained inflation.
  • Corporate earnings and volume data from major companies like Nestle, Walmart, and a major French fry manufacturer show that consumers are cutting back at the margins. When Nestle attempted to raise prices in North America, its sales volumes fell by 0.6% instead of rising as projected, indicating that the consumer is reaching a breaking point.
  • The WTI oil futures curve is heavily backwardated, with front-month contracts screaming higher due to short-term supply constraints while long-term contracts (into 2026 and 2027) remain flat. This indicates that the oil market itself does not expect high prices to last, anticipating that high near-term energy costs will destroy demand and force prices down.
  • Despite rising front-end oil prices, TIPS breakeven rates have barely moved, showing that the inflation-protection market is pricing the energy spike as a temporary CPI blip rather than a long-term inflationary trend. Similarly, the rise in the two-year Treasury yield is driven purely by the risk of the Fed irrationally raising rates, not by expectations of economic growth.
  • The interest rate swap market has decoupled from the Fed's hawkish rhetoric, with short-run swap spreads turning more negative even as nominal yields rise. This suggests that while the Fed may hike rates in the short term, the market expects them to pivot and aggressively cut rates within a few months once the labor market begins to break.

Why you Need to Understand the Steady Bid for U.S. Assets that is Preventing the Market's fall...
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

The U.S. stock market is supported by a relentless, price-insensitive "steady bid" driven by passive management, automatic retirement contributions, and massive foreign capital inflows seeking safety and tax advantages. However, this same passive mechanism can work in reverse during periods of rising unemployment or global carry trade unwinds, potentially causing rapid market drawdowns. Short-term technical indicators suggest momentum is cooling off for major indexes, while extreme commercial short positions in copper present a tactical short-term trading opportunity despite strong long-term fundamentals.

Key points
  • The rise of passive management has created an automatic, price-insensitive bid for U.S. equities, with passive funds now making up over 50% of equity funds compared to less than 20% two decades ago. This algorithmic buying occurs automatically whenever cash enters the system, regardless of valuations or underlying fundamentals.
  • Automatic retirement contributions from approximately 70 million active 401(k) and IRA accounts provide a continuous flow of capital into target-date and index funds, meaning the steady bid remains highly dependent on the health of the U.S. labor market. If unemployment rises and payrolls fall, this bid disappears and can quickly turn into net selling as out-of-work individuals tap their retirement savings.
  • Foreign capital heavily favors U.S. assets due to geopolitical instability abroad, the depth of U.S. markets, and the fact that the U.S. acts as a major offshore tax haven. The U.S. does not reciprocate financial reporting under the same standards it demands from over 110 other countries, making it highly attractive for foreign wealth preservation. Additionally, the rise of stablecoins and asset tokenization has lowered the barrier to entry, allowing emerging market retail investors to buy U.S. assets with as little as $5.
  • The same passive mechanism that drives the market upward can accelerate market crashes on the downside. When net redemptions occur, passive funds must sell assets in proportion to their size without regard to price, which historically has compressed what would normally be slow, multi-month drawdowns into rapid multi-week panics, as seen in 2008, 2020, and 2025.
  • Tactical market indicators show that while the S&P 500 and NASDAQ have broken below their 50-day moving averages, their stochastics are already heavily oversold, suggesting a limited near-term downside before testing the 100-day or 200-day moving averages. Meanwhile, copper is sitting at its 50-day moving average with commercial short positions at historic highs, signaling a potential short-term pullback despite a bullish long-term outlook.
