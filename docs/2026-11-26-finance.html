Key points
  • Gold recently broke its three-year support trendline and lost key price support levels, matching patterns that preceded 35% declines during the market peaks of 1981 and 2011–2013.
  • The CME altered paper gold margin requirements by abandoning fixed-dollar amounts in favor of a dynamic 5% to 9% percentage system, forcing institutional investors and hedge funds into massive liquidations and pushing open interest to multi-year lows.
  • Physical demand for gold remains near record highs at 5,000 tons annually—outstripping annual supply of 4,900 tons—driven primarily by price-insensitive global central bank purchases despite a decade-long drought in major new mining discoveries.
  • Short-term pressures on gold are compounded by Federal Reserve interest rate hikes, which increase borrowing costs for margin traders and raise yield competition from U.S. Treasury bonds.
  • Emerging stagflation conditions—evidenced by 3.7% inflation paired with rapidly slowing GDP growth—could ultimately benefit gold, as demonstrated by gold's 20-fold rise during the rate-hiking cycles of the 1970s.
