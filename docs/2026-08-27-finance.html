How the US Killed Japan's Economy. And, why its coming for India next?

TLDR

The Japanese yen carry trade, where investors borrow cheaply in Japan to buy US assets, is a key driver of the US stock market that is unlikely to collapse anytime soon due to persistent interest rate differentials. Japan is forced to keep its interest rates low because of its massive debt-to-GDP ratio and low-growth economy. While India is not a capital source for the US like Japan, the US is successfully capturing India's high-end consumer market, draining high-margin wealth from the country.

Key points
  • The Japanese yen carry trade is an arbitrage model where institutional investors borrow yen at extremely low interest rates from the Bank of Japan, convert it to US dollars, and invest in higher-yielding US assets like equities, bonds, and real estate.
  • Concerns about the unwinding of the yen carry trade are often overstated because the Bank of Japan cannot easily raise interest rates. With a debt-to-GDP ratio of 225%, any significant rate hike would exponentially increase Japan's debt repayment obligations and further stall its low-growth economy.
  • Historically, Japan's economic stagnation began in the 1990s due to an aging demographic, a technology shift, and a massive accumulation of debt taken on during its previous high-growth era.
  • Japan's monetary policy is highly dependent on the US Federal Reserve; the Bank of Japan lacks full independence and must mirror US rate movements to prevent destabilizing capital outflows.
  • Unlike Japan, India is not a major source of capital for the US, but it is a consumption-led economy where the US is successfully capturing high-end, high-margin consumer spending through digital platforms, higher education, and luxury goods.
  • While India runs a goods trade surplus with the US, the overall balance flips in favor of the US when accounting for services, education, digital platforms, and defense purchases, which poses a long-term risk to domestic Indian margins.

Key points
  • Hyperinflation is fundamentally a currency event driven by a loss of confidence in money itself, rather than a purely economic event, as demonstrated by historical precedents in 1790s France and 1920s Weimar Germany.
  • The primary driver of hyperinflation is unsustainable national debt, which leads governments to buy back their own debt by issuing even more debt, a pattern currently visible in US Treasury buyback operations.
  • The US dollar's status as the global reserve currency makes the current situation unprecedented, especially as the US Treasury threatens to kick non-compliant nations out of the dollar system, alienating foreign buyers of US debt.
  • According to Austrian economist Ludwig von Mises, a "crack-up boom" occurs when the general public realizes inflation is a deliberate, endless policy, triggering a sudden rush to swap cash for real goods before the currency becomes worthless.
  • Physical gold and silver represent real cash and specy, offering a liquid, safe-haven alternative to irredeemable paper and digital fiat currencies before the window of opportunity closes.
