WTF Just Happened to Retail Sales?!

TLDR

The July retail sales contraction, along with declining auto sales and gasoline usage, is not a one-off calendar distortion but rather the result of predictable economic mini-cycles. This downturn is driven by a weakening labor market, falling real incomes, depleted consumer savings, and high energy costs. The data suggests the economy is highly fragile and may be transitioning from a series of mini-cycles into a more prolonged recessionary business cycle.

Key points
  • The decline in July retail sales is part of a predictable pattern where artificial springtime highs—boosted by tax refunds and front-running inflation—give way to summer slowdowns once consumer funds run out.
  • A weakening labor market is the primary driver behind the spending drop, as evidenced by a sharp decline in average hourly earnings for rank-and-file workers, which historically leads to reduced retail sales and subsequent layoffs.
  • Consumer savings rates have plummeted to levels not seen since the 1940s, leaving the average consumer with no financial margin to sustain spending in the face of persistent inflation and high energy costs.
  • Mainstream explanations blaming the retail sales drop on seasonal calendar distortions, such as the timing of Amazon Prime Day, ignore broader systemic declines in auto sales and peak-season gasoline demand.
  • Small and medium-sized businesses are facing a squeeze from rising input and energy costs that they cannot pass on to struggling consumers, forcing them to cut labor expenses and hours.

The Fort Knox Gold Problem Nobody Considers...
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

The ongoing controversy over whether the United States' gold is actually in Fort Knox persists because a full, independent audit of individual bars has not occurred since 1953. While official audits verify intact wax seals on the vault compartments annually, skeptics argue the gold may be leased, swapped, or replaced with gold-plated bars. However, the weight of evidence suggests the gold is present, and the U.S. may even possess undocumented surplus gold acquired from historical global conflicts.

Key points
  • Fort Knox was built in 1936 to secure gold seized from U.S. citizens after the metal was demonetized and repriced, and it currently holds the largest portion of the nation's 245 million fine troy ounces of gold.
  • The Federal Reserve Bank of New York acts as a custodian for approximately 6,000 tons of gold belonging to foreign governments, which undermines theories of U.S. gold depletion since foreign nations continue to trust the U.S. with their own physical reserves.
  • Official annual audits conducted by the Treasury Department do not weigh or assay individual gold bars; instead, they verify the integrity of pre-numbered wax seals on the 42 vault compartment doors.
  • Conspiracy theories suggest the gold has been secretly sold, leased, or swapped, fueled by historical government gold price manipulation like the London Gold Pool and the fact that the government cannot locate several audit records from 1975 to 1986.
  • An alternative theory suggests Fort Knox could hold a surplus of gold rather than a shortfall, as the U.S. military may have recovered and quietly stored undocumented gold from Europe and Asia following World War II.
  • Despite calls for a comprehensive public audit, the U.S. government is unlikely to conduct one due to the high cost, potential security risks of exposing the facility, and the reality that a vocal minority of skeptics will remain unconvinced regardless of the results.
