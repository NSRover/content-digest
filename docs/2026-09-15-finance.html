TLDR

The global financial system is undergoing a "Great Financial Reset," a structural transformation comparable to the 1970s, driven by the maturation of the digital economy and new financial innovations like programmable money and stablecoins. While the dollar's dominance is being challenged by geopolitical shifts and the rise of independent financial rails, its deep network effects and liquidity suggest its reserve currency status will persist for a long time. Investors should understand how these shifts, including the emergence of machine-to-machine commerce, will reshape the monetary order.

Key points
  • The core innovation in "crypto" is programmable money and smart contracts, which allow for peer-to-peer value transfer and the automatic execution of financial and legal agreements, extending to the tokenization of various assets. This merges the legal and financial layers, enabling new forms of automated transactions.
  • The current "Great Financial Reset" is a structural shift in the global monetary order, similar to the 1970s when the end of Bretton Woods and new financial innovations like junk bonds and passive investing emerged. This reset is driven by the maturity of the "semiconductors, computing, and networks" techno-economic paradigm.
  • The dollar's dominance, despite challenges, is rooted in its widespread decentralization through systems like the petrodollar and eurodollar, strong network effects, and deep liquidity provided by US public debt. These factors make it the default currency for global transactions, even as other currencies gain traction in trade.
  • Modern drivers of this financial reset include the 2008 crisis and Bitcoin's emergence, the 2014 Crimea invasion prompting countries like China to build independent financial systems, the COVID-19 pandemic accelerating digitalization, and the Trump administration's use of technology as an instrument of dollar power.
  • While there is growing uncertainty about the pace of dollar-denominated stablecoin adoption and the long-term maintenance of US financial hegemony, the dollar's reserve currency status is expected to decline slowly, potentially over decades, similar to the British pound's historical trajectory.
  • The emerging "agentic economy" and machine-to-machine commerce highlight a need for new financial infrastructure, as existing systems are poorly suited for AI agents. Stablecoins are seen as a potential default payment mechanism in this evolving landscape.

Inflation Isn’t a Mistake — It’s Built Into the System.

TLDR

Inflation is not an accident but a deliberate government policy designed to sustain a debt-based fiat monetary system. Since the end of the gold standard in 1971, the M2 money supply has grown at a compound annual rate of 6.6%, far outpacing the official consumer price index and resulting in a massive debasement of the dollar. To survive the looming treasury and debt crisis, the Federal Reserve and the Treasury may ultimately be forced to resort to radical measures, such as revaluing the statutory price of gold.

Key points
  • The classic definition of inflation—an abnormal increase in the volume of money and credit leading to rising prices—has been discarded by policymakers and media in favor of tracking the Consumer Price Index (CPI), which obscures the true source of currency debasement.
  • A historical analysis of the M2 money supply shows continuous growth regardless of which political party occupies the White House, proving that monetary expansion is a structural feature of the system rather than a partisan issue.
  • While the M2 money supply briefly contracted between 2022 and late 2023, prices continued to rise because the massive liquidity injected during the 2020 crisis was too large to be offset by a temporary period of monetary tightening.
  • Since 1971, M2 has grown at an average annual rate of 6.6% compared to the official CPI average of 3.9%, meaning the real rate of inflation has compounded 70% faster than official government metrics admit.
  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's $4 billion bond-buyback program, funded by issuing short-term debt, has been compared by analysts to paying a mortgage with a credit card, signaling a dangerous watershed moment for the stability of the U.S. Treasury market.
  • Because the modern economy is entirely dependent on inflated financial assets and expanding debt, the Federal Reserve cannot allow a prolonged bear market or a contraction of the money supply without risking systemic collapse.
