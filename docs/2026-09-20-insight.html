William Costello is a psychology researcher and Ph.D. student specializing in evolutionary psychology. He is the winner of the Feminist Evolutionary Psychology Society (FEPS) Award and is currently writing a paper titled "The Mismeasurement of Men" addressing scales that pathologize male attitudes.

TLDR

The intense backlash against actress Sydney Sweeney's sports betting advertisement highlights deep-seated hypocrisies within modern feminism and is rooted in evolutionary psychology, specifically female intrasexual competition and sexual economics. Critics police Sweeney's appeal to the "male gaze" because she bypasses female-centric solidarity, contrasting sharply with how other sexualized female artists are celebrated. Ultimately, this hyper-focus on her physical appearance and perceived threat to women's sports distracts from the genuinely corrosive societal impact of the sports gambling product she is promoting to young men.

Key points
  • The backlash claiming Sydney Sweeney's sports betting ad set women's sports back 40 years is absurd and reflects a low opinion of female athletics, especially given the historical acceptance of other female athletes posing naked for major publications.
  • The criticism reveals a tribal double standard where self-sexualization is deemed empowering when performed by progressive "in-group" figures like Sabrina Carpenter, but is condemned as pandering when done by someone like Sweeney who directly appeals to the male gaze without first seeking female approval.
  • Evolutionary psychology explains female resentment toward Sweeney through intrasexual competition and sexual economics theory, where women act as a cartel to keep the "price" of sexual access high; Sweeney's direct appeal to male desires is policed because it threatens to lower this market price.
  • Unlike men, who generally view highly attractive or formidable men as aspirational allies, women are evolutionary primed to view highly attractive peers as direct threats to their mating security, leading to social policing, "tall poppy syndrome," and the shunning of those who do not downplay their own assets.
  • Sydney Sweeney's refusal to adopt a victim narrative or frame her physical development as a struggle-to-liberation arc alienates her from mainstream feminist solidarity, which struggles to conceptualize female empowerment outside of classic high-status male roles or collective political victimhood.
  • The outrage over Sweeney's physical appearance has completely overshadowed the actual societal harm of the advertisement: the promotion of sports gambling, which research shows leads to a predictable rise in bankruptcy, debt, and high rates of suicidality among young men trying to rapidly increase their mate value.
  • Society exhibits a "gamma bias" and a general lack of empathy for male-specific harms, meaning critics chose to attack Sweeney's ad through the lens of female victimhood rather than launching a far more persuasive collective action against the predatory nature of gambling targeted at young men.
