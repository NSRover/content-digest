Something Extremely Rare Just Happened to the Yield Curve
gemini-flash (gemini-2.5-flash)fallback
TLDR

The Treasury yield curve is currently exhibiting a rare shape, steep at the front and flat at the back, a combination seen in only 5.4% of trading days since 1990. This unusual configuration signals a significant disagreement between the Federal Reserve's hawkish stance and the bond market's skepticism about future economic growth and sustained inflation. Historically, this curve shape has appeared near policy and economic transitions, often preceding a reversal of central bank policy.

Key points
  • The yield curve's current shape is characterized by a steep front (3-month to 2-year spread of at least 60 basis points) and a flat back (2-year to 10-year spread of 31 basis points or lower), a combination that has occurred on only about 5.4% of trading days since 1990.
  • This rare curve shape indicates that the market expects the Federal Reserve to continue raising short-term rates, but it does not believe these hikes are validated by stronger long-term economic prospects or sustained inflation.
  • Market-based inflation expectations, derived from TIPS, remain subdued and do not show a meaningful inflation scare, even with rising energy prices, suggesting inflation is not the primary driver of rising nominal yields.
  • Stronger long-term growth expectations are also not driving the current curve shape; a genuine improvement in growth prospects would typically lead to a steepening of the back end of the curve, which is not happening now.
  • Historical instances of this curve shape, such as in 2000, before 2008, and in late 2018, have consistently appeared when markets believed central bankers were inappropriately hawkish while underlying economic conditions were deteriorating, often leading to policy reversals.
  • The steep front of the curve reflects the market pricing in the Federal Reserve's likely reaction to recent economic data and potential headline inflation from energy prices, while the flat back reflects concerns about future growth and the eventual reversal of these rate hikes.

Could Britain Be the Next Iceland? The Gilt-Sterling Doom Loop.
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

The United Kingdom faces a growing risk of a gilt-sterling "doom loop" and a potential currency crisis similar to Iceland's in 2008. Because the UK bond market is heavily reliant on foreign investors and the Bank of England, falling bond prices and a weakening pound could feed into each other, triggering severe inflation and a collapse in living standards. Investors are urged to consider gold and silver to protect their wealth from a potential currency devaluation.

Key points
  • The UK's financial situation differs from Iceland's pre-2008 banking sector, but it faces a similar currency risk where a rapid devaluation could double the cost of living essentials.
  • Ownership of UK government bonds has shifted dramatically since 2008, with domestic pension funds and insurers dropping their share from 46% to just 20% in 2026, leaving the market highly dependent on foreigners (34%) and the Bank of England (17%).
  • A self-created energy crisis, particularly the UK's reliance on importing 54% of its diesel, threatens to paralyze the economy and halt the credit system, serving as a potential trigger for financial collapse.
  • The "doom loop" occurs as rising bond yields increase borrowing costs across the economy; if the Bank of England prints money to buy bonds and suppress yields, it will further depreciate the pound and accelerate inflation.
  • The British pound is showing signs of a downward trend against the dollar, with 1.30 identified as a key level to watch for a potential decline.
  • Physical gold and silver serve as vital hedges against currency crashes because gold is the only currency that carries no associated financial obligations.
