Americans Are Running Out of Money. The Data Is Getting Ugly.
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Federal Reserve survey data reveals that consumer anxiety regarding the labor market has reached its highest level since April 2020, with 44.4% of respondents expecting unemployment to rise over the next year. This fear of job loss and prolonged unemployment is driving defensive consumer behavior, leading to demand destruction rather than an inflationary spiral. Despite rising costs for necessities, overall inflation expectations are declining as households cut back on discretionary spending and limit credit card debt.

Key points
  • The perceived probability among consumers that the unemployment rate will rise in one year has climbed to 44.4%, a level of labor market anxiety not seen since the April 2020 pandemic crisis. Additionally, consumers believe that finding a new job within three months of a layoff has become exceptionally difficult, mirroring sentiment from 2012.
  • Defensive consumer behavior is highly visible in the housing market, where existing home sales fell below an annualized rate of 4 million in August. This decline persists despite falling mortgage rates and a 5.9% year-over-year increase in previously owned home inventory, indicating that buyers are avoiding long-term financial commitments due to income insecurity.
  • While consumers expect prices for necessities like food, rent, and gasoline to rise, their overall median one-year inflation expectations declined for a third consecutive month to 3.58%. This demonstrates that households view price shocks as a drain on their discretionary purchasing power rather than a trigger for a broader, self-reinforcing inflationary spiral.
  • Consumer credit data from July shows a stark divergence between revolving and non-revolving debt. While total credit rose by 15 billion, revolving credit (primarily credit cards) grew by only 2.8 billion, signaling that households are highly reluctant to use open-ended debt to finance ordinary spending amidst labor market uncertainty.
  • The bond market aligns with consumer sentiment rather than the Federal Reserve's hawkish stance. Historically flat yield curves and stable long-term inflation break-evens indicate that investors expect the collision of high energy prices and a weak labor market to result in disinflationary demand destruction rather than sustained inflation.

China Is Taking HALF the World's Gold Supply — What Happens Next? | Eric Robinson
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

China is consuming roughly five tons of physical gold per day, which represents about half of the global mining industry's daily production of ten tons. Despite a quadrupling of the gold price since its late 2015 low, global mine supply has actually fallen and cannot quickly respond to higher prices due to the five to ten years required to build new mines. This supply-side constraint, combined with an unravelling global sovereign debt crisis, makes pre-IPO gold and strategic mineral investments highly attractive.

Key points
  • Global gold production from mines has fallen since the late 2015 price low, even though the price of gold has quadrupled over the same period. Building a decent mine with necessary infrastructure and processing facilities takes five to ten years and hundreds of millions of dollars, meaning supply cannot quickly catch up to demand.
  • In-house research by Chancery Mining Group indicates that China is purchasing approximately five tons of physical gold per day. Because the entire global mining industry only produces about ten tons of gold per day, China is effectively absorbing half of the world's daily gold supply.
  • The price of raw tungsten has increased tenfold in approximately 18 months due to a lack of near-term supply and China's control of 80% to 85% of the global market. Western governments are scrambling to secure the metal, which is critical for defense, manufacturing, and artificial intelligence applications.
  • Unlike other commodities, tungsten has no paper futures or options market, meaning its price is driven entirely by physical supply and demand and cannot be manipulated by banks or financial elites.
  • Chancery Mining Group utilizes a pre-IPO model to raise private equity funds for undervalued, past-producing mines that are close to restarting production. This model allows private investors to acquire shares at a low valuation before the companies are listed on public stock exchanges.
  • The global sovereign debt crisis is actively unravelling, having started with defaults in peripheral nations like Zambia, Lebanon, Argentina, Ecuador, and Sri Lanka, and is now moving toward core Western economies.
