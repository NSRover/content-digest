The Internet Will Die and So Will You | John West

long-form summary compiled from 3 transcript segments (map-reduce)

TLDR

The internet is a deeply ephemeral space subject to decay and "link rot" rather than a permanent archive. Over time, the web has transitioned from a generative, user-driven space of open standards to a highly centralized ecosystem dominated by algorithmic platforms that prioritize advertiser revenue over human agency. This shift has eroded shared societal norms, accelerated epistemic collapse, and replaced genuine human connection with engagement-driven monetization.

Key points
  • The internet is inherently impermanent and prone to decay, a reality illustrated by "link rot" where personal websites, blogs, and digital archives routinely disappear or redirect to unrelated commercial sites.
  • The digital landscape has shifted from a pastoral, user-controlled space of personal homepages and open standards like RSS to a highly centralized environment dominated by closed platform feeds.
  • Modern social media platforms have transitioned from serving their users to serving advertisers, treating human attention and data as commodities to be monetized through engagement-driven algorithms.
  • High switching costs and algorithmic mediation create a state of digital displacement, where users lack ownership over the online communities and spaces they inhabit.
  • The rise of engagement-based algorithms has contributed to epistemic collapse and the spread of conspiracy theories, as platforms prioritize outrage and "vice signaling" over truth and constructive knowledge production.
  • Framing digital moderation strictly as a debate between free speech and censorship is flawed because content-neutral algorithms inherently amplify or suppress information to maximize user engagement.

The Most Important Gold Chart in the World Is Warning Us.

TLDR

The long-term debasement of the U.S. dollar and other major fiat currencies since the closing of the gold window in 1971 highlights the necessity of holding physical gold as financial insurance. The suspension of the Bretton Woods gold standard initiated a secular bear market for fiat currencies, which have lost over 90% of their value. As global monetary instability rises and new gold discoveries remain scarce, physical gold serves as essential protection against currency collapse and counterparty risk.

Key points
  • On August 15, 1971, President Nixon suspended the convertibility of the U.S. dollar into gold at the fixed rate of $35 per troy ounce, effectively defaulting on the Bretton Woods agreement and initiating the era of floating fiat currencies.
  • Since 1971, all major fiat currencies have lost more than 90% of their value relative to gold, demonstrating that holding paper currency over the long term is disastrous for savers looking to preserve wealth.
  • Prior to 1971, the gold anchor provided price stability, whereas the post-1971 unbacked fiat system has led to massive government spending, financialization of the economy, and the de-industrialization of the United States.
  • The supply side of gold is becoming increasingly critical because there have been no major gold discoveries for approximately 20 years, and rising global demand—such as China's consumption of 1,000 tons annually—will make physical gold harder to obtain.
  • Principles from *A Pocketbook of Gold* emphasize that physical gold is not a commodity or an investment to be traded, but rather essential financial insurance that eliminates counterparty risk, whereas gold ETFs defeat this purpose and carry significant risks.
  • The Laiva Gold mine in Finland, which features the largest gold mill in Europe with a crushing capacity of 500 tons per hour, is preparing to resume production and is projected to produce just under 1.6 tons of gold per year.
