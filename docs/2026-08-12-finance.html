While a weakening US dollar could push nominal stock prices higher, it is expected to bring extreme market volatility where passive investing fails and systematic strategies become essential.

Key points
  • US gold exports have surged fivefold over the past year, rising from a decade-long average of $10 billion per year to nearly $50 billion, with China acting as a primary buyer.
  • China has reduced its holdings of US treasuries to an 18-year low, while central banks globally purchased 345 tons of gold, making gold their largest reserve asset and surpassing US treasuries for the first time.
  • The 50-year-old global financial loop—where the US ran trade deficits and foreign nations recycled their dollars back into US treasuries—is cracking because the model hollowed out the US middle class and concentrated wealth in financial assets.
  • In response to domestic political pressure, US policymakers have implemented tariffs, sanctions, and industrial policies since 2018 to bring manufacturing back home, removing the incentive for foreign nations to hold US dollar-based assets.
  • Historical precedents, such as the collapse of the British pound sterling, suggest that a declining reserve currency can lead to rising nominal stock prices as the currency loses purchasing power, as seen in the UK and during the US in the 1930s, 1940s, and 1970s.
  • Holding cash during this transition is highly risky, but passive investing is also likely to fail due to violent market swings, making systematic investment strategies necessary to navigate the volatility and build wealth.

Key points
  • Senator Rand Paul's public statements during his Fort Knox visit contradicted official U.S. gold reserve figures. He first stated the U.S. has about 147 million ounces of gold, and later posted an incorrect correction claiming the country has 477 million ounces, whereas the official Treasury figure has stood at 261.5 million troy ounces (8,133.5 metric tons) for decades.
  • Critics of the official narrative argue that even if the physical gold is present in Fort Knox, it may be encumbered through leasing agreements. Central banks and institutions like the New York Fed and the BIS have historically leased gold to bullion banks to manipulate paper markets and suppress prices, meaning the gold may be legally claimed by other parties.
  • Historical precedents, such as the three years it took the New York Fed to repatriate 300 tons of gold to Germany's Bundesbank, fuel skepticism about the immediate availability and unencumbered status of U.S. gold reserves.
  • Government accounting standards, specifically FASAB 56 approved in 2017, allow the U.S. government and major corporations to keep certain financial activities off the books for national security reasons, making a truly transparent and independent audit of the gold reserves highly unlikely.
  • While some commentators argue that questioning the gold reserves is part of a psychological warfare campaign to discredit U.S. institutions, proponents of sound money maintain that a full, independent audit is the only way to restore public trust and expose potential market manipulation.
