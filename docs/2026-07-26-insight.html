Guest

Dr. Stephen Porges is a neuroscientist, psychologist, and the creator of the Polyvagal Theory. He has authored more than 400 peer-reviewed scientific papers and has spent decades researching the autonomic nervous system, heart rate variability, and the evolutionary mechanisms of human connection and defense.

TLDR

The Polyvagal Theory explains how our underlying physiological state dictates our perception of safety, threat, and social connection. When the nervous system is locked in chronic defense due to trauma, illness, or stress, it impairs our ability to think clearly, relate to others, and maintain physical health. By understanding the evolutionary pathways of the vagus nerve, we can use targeted physiological interventions—such as sound protocols, resonant breathing, and metabolic support—to signal safety to the body and restore autonomic flexibility.

Key points
  • The Polyvagal Theory posits that our physiological state acts as a lens through which we perceive the world, meaning our feelings of anxiety or safety are driven by bodily states rather than external narratives. The autonomic nervous system evolved in three stages: a primitive dorsal vagal system (immobilization and shutdown), a sympathetic system (mobilization and fight-or-flight), and a newer mammalian ventral vagal system (social engagement and co-regulation).
  • True safety is not merely the absence of threat, but the active presence of safety signals, such as melodic vocalizations and warm facial expressions. For individuals with a history of trauma, the nervous system can paradoxically associate these positive cues of connection with vulnerability and danger, triggering defensive reactions to safe situations.
  • Chronic illnesses like long COVID, chronic fatigue, and Lyme disease can lock the nervous system into a persistent state of defense, reducing autonomic flexibility. This state can be rehabilitated not by "hacking" the system, but by gradually challenging it and using acoustic interventions like the Safe and Sound Protocol, which uses modulated frequencies to mimic a mother's calming voice.
  • The biggest myth about anxiety is that it is caused by external events, when it is actually the body broadcasting its internal physiological defensive state. When the body is stuck in this defensive mode, higher cognitive functions are compromised, limiting a person's capacity to solve complex problems or experience empathy.
  • The vagus nerve serves as a bidirectional surveillance system, with 80% of its fibers sending sensory information from the body—especially the gut—up to the brain. Because of this close connection, a highly dysregulated autonomic nervous system is frequently the underlying cause of gastrointestinal issues that lack a clear physical pathology.
  • Metabolic support is a crucial prerequisite for nervous system rehabilitation, as the body requires sufficient resources like mitochondrial ATP and neurotransmitters to respond to somatic interventions. Without adequate metabolic fuel, the nervous system remains unable to transition out of survival mode.
  • Stellate Ganglion Blocks (SGBs) can help individuals locked in chronic fight-or-flight by dampening the sympathetic nervous system, allowing the parasympathetic ventral vagus to express itself. However, this intervention is primarily effective for hyperarousal states and may not assist those stuck in a dorsal vagal shutdown or immobilization state.
