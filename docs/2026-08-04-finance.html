The AI Enshittification Bubble | Cory Doctorow

TLDR

The AI boom is a structurally unprofitable bubble driven by unsustainable unit economics, where companies are effectively selling "$100 bills for a dollar" and using opaque accounting to mask massive inference costs. Unlike early web companies that benefited from scale, frontier AI models face low switching costs and escalating training expenses, forcing tech giants to rely on runaway growth narratives to prop up their valuations. While smaller, specialized AI models may eventually hold real value, the current capital expenditure bubble led by large foundation models is poised to collapse once investor capital runs out.

Key points
  • The concept of "enshittification" describes a three-stage platform life cycle where tech companies first lock in users, then exploit those users to benefit business customers, and finally claw back all value for themselves, a cycle enabled by the dismantling of competition, regulation, and worker bargaining power over the last 25 years.
  • Unlike the early web, which possessed strong unit economics that improved with scale, the AI industry is structurally unprofitable because every new user and subsequent generation of technology costs more to serve and train, resulting in inverted unit economics.
  • AI companies are allegedly using opaque accounting standards outside of GAAP to fabricate profitability, such as hiding massive inference costs on their balance sheets by mislabeling them as marketing expenses.
  • The lack of high switching costs between frontier AI models like ChatGPT and Claude prevents companies from establishing a sustainable moat, forcing them into a continuous, hyper-expensive capital expenditure race to train the next model just to survive.
  • Tech giants rely on runaway growth narratives—transitioning from web3 and the metaverse to AI—to maintain high price-to-earnings ratios and use their inflated stock as liquid capital for acquisitions and hiring.
  • The impact of AI on labor is defined by the struggle between "centaurs" (humans empowered by machines) and "reverse centaurs" (humans controlled by machines), with many workers being forced into the latter role to act as "accountability syncs" who sign off on unchecked, AI-generated tech debt.
  • While the massive capital expenditure bubble surrounding large foundation models and big tech is highly likely to burst, actual long-term value may still accrue in smaller, specialized AI models.
