A Divorce Lawyer’s Advice: Stop Keeping Score

On Chris Williamson’s Mostly Wise panel, New York divorce attorney James Sexton argues that the clearest early sign of a marriage in trouble is not a fight but an absence: the small, unasked-for kindnesses quietly stop. Sexton’s prescription is care offered before it is requested, and a warning that keeping score breeds resentment — judge a partner on the sum of the days, not any single one, and make peace with the quirks. The panel, with comedians Matt McCusker and Rick Glassman, also argues for raising hard subjects as a shared concern rather than a complaint. Why it matters to us: with the baby four weeks away, sleep and patience will run short, and tallies of who did the last night feed come easily. One lawyer’s view from practice, not research. — Chris Williamson, “Mostly Wise #4”, content digest

Deep Dive — Is the bond market’s plumbing wearing thin?

The commentator behind maneco64 argues that the US Treasury market — the collateral under much of the world’s short-term lending — is getting harder to trade. US debt has grown from about $3 trillion in the early 1990s to $40 trillion, but the number of dealers making a market in it has not, so spreads are widening even on the newest, most-traded bonds. The danger, the channel says, is the “basis trade”, in which hedge funds borrow up to 56 times their stake to profit from tiny gaps between bonds and futures. A sudden spike in volatility could force them out together and freeze the repo market, as briefly happened in 2019. It also flags that over-the-counter derivatives remain far less regulated than exchange-traded ones, so the leverage is hard to see. The channel’s remedy is gold, money with no counterparty — which, it says, is why countries such as China keep buying it. One commentator’s view, not a forecast. — maneco64, “The Bond Market’s Hidden Time Bomb”, content digest
