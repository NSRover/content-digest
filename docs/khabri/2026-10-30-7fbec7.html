Why Waiting Feels Hard When Nothing Is Wrong

One day past the due date, with nothing on the calendar until Monday’s yoga, the most fitting listen of the week is psychologist Rob Kurzban on Chris Williamson’s podcast. His argument: emotions are evolved gauges, not faults. Anxiety is the brain’s manager of future risk — it weighs what might go wrong and pushes us to prepare — and modern life, with its constant inputs, tends to over-trigger these ancient systems. Even the uncomfortable feelings have a job, he says: guilt and embarrassment protect our standing with others, and low mood can be a signal that asks the people around us for support. Knowing what a feeling is for makes it easier to handle. The household angle is plain: restlessness in the last days of waiting is the system doing its work, not a sign that something is wrong — and the midwife, not the clock, still decides when to call. — Chris Williamson (YouTube)

Pakketjes van buiten de EU (new fees on non-EU parcels)

▸ Ordering baby clothes or naming-ceremony items from an Indian webshop? Since 1 July, parcels under €150 carry €3 import duty per type of product — a wool and a cotton jumper already count twice. From Sunday 1 November a further €2 per declaration line is added, also above €150, non-refundable even if the parcel is refused, with VAT on top. The webshop or the courier (PostNL, DHL) passes the cost on; the Douane itself never sends a payment request, so a text or mail asking you to pay “the Douane” is fake. Bundle orders into few product types; a real gift from family up to €45 stays duty-free.from Sun 1 Nov 2026 — douane.nl, verified 30 Oct

De Kleine Khabri — Week 40 + 1

Past the due date, the baby simply carries on: still putting on a little weight, lungs and brain still maturing, the protective white coating on the skin thinning — which is why babies born late often have drier, peeling skin for a few days, harmlessly. Most first babies who are not here by 40 weeks arrive within the following fortnight on their own. In the Netherlands the midwife usually checks in more often from around 41 weeks and may offer “strippen” (a membrane sweep) to help labour start; induction is discussed from 41 weeks too. The rule stays the same: regular contractions, waters breaking, blood loss or fewer movements mean a call to the midwife.

Deep Dive — An AI credit bust?

On MacroVoices, Luke Gromen argues that AI-driven white-collar layoffs threaten the roughly $19 trillion of US consumer debt: every five-point rise in default rates would mean about $1 trillion of losses, some 40% of US banks’ equity capital. In a recession that shock, he says, would push annual US deficits from about $2 trillion to $4–5 trillion, and with hedge funds and foreign central banks stepping back, the Federal Reserve would end up printing money to absorb the new Treasury debt. He keeps 30% of his liquid net worth in cash and T-bills, and points to gold’s support near $4,000. One commentator’s case, not a forecast. — MacroVoices #556
