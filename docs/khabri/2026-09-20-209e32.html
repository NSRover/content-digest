Deep Dive — Why diesel is the price to watch

Diesel is the fuel the industrial economy actually runs on, and the shortage now driving it is not about crude but about refining. Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries, a Russian export ban and damaged capacity in the Middle East have all cut finished-fuel supply, and a refinery cannot simply make more diesel — every extra litre comes out of petrol. The American retail average has already reached a record, and the argument is that eight, nine or ten dollars a gallon is a genuine risk. What follows is the part that reaches a household: the two-year Treasury yield has reconnected to the oil price, so the bond market has begun pricing in more rate rises — even though no interest rate can build a refinery. The obvious remedy is worse than it looks, too. Energy companies warn that banning diesel exports would remove the reason to refine at home and push petrol up by fifteen to thirty cents a gallon instead. The 1990 oil shock is the precedent: energy spikes do not inflate forever, they destroy demand and take employment down with them, and the tightening arrives just as the economy can least afford it. — Eurodollar University, content digest

De Kleine Khabri — Week 34, dag 3

Five weeks and four days to go. The baby is around 44 centimetres now and gaining something like 200 grams a week, most of it fat laid down under the skin — which is why the wrinkled look of a month ago has been filling out and the skin has turned from red to pink. The bones have all hardened except the skull, which stays deliberately soft and unfused so it can overlap on the way out. Hearing is fully developed at this stage: voices heard now are recognised after birth, which is the whole argument for talking and reading aloud at a pregnant belly rather than feeling silly about it. The lungs remain the last thing to finish. On Saturday 10 October comes vroege termijn — the thirty-seven-week line past which an arrival counts as full-term rather than early.
