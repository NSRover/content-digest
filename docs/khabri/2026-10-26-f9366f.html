Less Movement or Bleeding: Call the Midwife at Once

Three days from the due date, this is the one rule worth knowing by heart. Call the midwife or gynaecologist straight away if the baby moves less than usual, or not at all; if there is any bleeding; or if the waters break and the fluid is yellow, green or brown — and if the baby has not yet dropped, lie down. Also call when contractions seem to have started or the waters break, as agreed with the midwife. The midwives’ association says there is no set number of kicks: what counts is less than normal. In doubt, lie on the left side with a hand on the bump; still uneasy, ring at once, even at night — there is no need to wait for morning. Why it matters to us: put the practice’s number in both phones today. Information, not medical advice — the midwife’s own arrangements come first. — deverloskundige.nl (KNOV) & thuisarts.nl, verified 26 Oct

Bij 41 weken: afwachten of inleiden (past 41 weeks, wait or start labour)

▸ Only if the baby has not come by Thursday 5 November (41 weeks). Thuisarts says the choice is then yours: wait for labour to start by itself, or have it started — by a membrane sweep, breaking the waters, or a balloon or medicine (that last route means a hospital birth). “One choice is not better than the other.” Waiting means extra checks: blood pressure, the baby’s heartbeat, a scan of the fluid; three in four women go into labour by themselves before 42 weeks. The same page notes a slightly higher chance of problems for the baby when waiting than with a balloon or medicine. Talk it over together and bring the questions to the 40–41-week check.from Thu 29 Oct; decide around Thu 5 Nov — thuisarts.nl, verified 26 Oct
