Bottle Feeding: Cold Tap Water, No Boiling Needed

Two days from the due date, one practical page worth reading before the kraamweek. If the baby gets formula, even some of the time, the Voedingscentrum’s steps are simple. Use cold tap water, never from the hot tap — Dutch tap water does not need boiling (if a house has lead pipes, use mineral water instead). Measure the powder with its own scoop, levelled off. Warm the bottle to no more than 30–35°C: a drop on the wrist should feel barely warm. Throw away anything left after half an hour. Make at most two bottles ahead, and keep them no longer than eight hours at the back of the fridge (4°C). For a healthy baby, sterilising is not needed — rinsing and a thorough wash are. Why it matters to us: night feeds come fast, and knowing the steps now saves guessing at 3 a.m. In doubt, ask the kraamverzorgende or the consultatiebureau. — voedingscentrum.nl, verified 27 Oct

Deep Dive — The AI spending boom, examined

On Hidden Forces, Paul Kedrosky argues the current AI investment cycle is unusually dangerous because it combines all four classic drivers of financial panics: loose credit, property speculation (data centres), technological euphoria and explicit government backstops. The data-centre build-out, he says, needs $4–6 trillion a year in revenue by the early 2030s — only reachable by repricing or replacing a large share of white-collar work, since AI mostly substitutes for that labour rather than complementing it. He notes that AI spending accounts for much of recent US growth and that the largest tech stocks make up a big slice of the S&P 500, so plain index funds are more exposed than they look. With governments already deep in debt, a crisis could mean austerity rather than rescue. Why it matters to us: one guest’s view, not advice — but a reminder of what a “broad” index fund actually holds. — Hidden Forces podcast (YouTube)
