Deep Dive — China swaps Treasuries for gold

China's holdings of US government debt have fallen to about 618 billion dollars, the lowest since 2008 and less than half the peak of late 2013. The selling accelerated after Washington froze Russia's reserves in 2022: if dollar reserves can be switched off, they are a liability as much as an asset. What Beijing buys instead is metal — official gold purchases have climbed through this year from 40,000 ounces in January to roughly twenty tonnes in August, with other central banks of the Global South following. The argument runs that a reserve currency losing its buyers ends up inflating, which is the long case for gold. A minority view, and a loud one, but the Treasury numbers themselves are not in dispute. — maneco64, content digest

De Kleine Khabri — Week 34, dag 2

Five weeks and five days to go. The lungs are still the long project, but the brain has taken over as the visible one: it is folding and wiring fast enough that sleep now comes in proper cycles, with stretches of rapid eye movement, and the baby practises breathing by drawing amniotic fluid in and out for hours at a time. Swallowing is rehearsal too. Iron is being stockpiled in the liver this month, enough to last roughly the first half-year outside; it is one reason the last weeks are worth having. Fingernails have reached the ends of the fingers. Three weeks from Saturday, on 10 October, comes the line called vroege termijn — thirty-seven weeks, the point from which an arrival counts as full-term rather than early.
