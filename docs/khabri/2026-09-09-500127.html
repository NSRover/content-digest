Deep Dive — The Sensitivity That Runs Hot

Dr Sasha Hamdani, a psychiatrist working on emotional regulation, tells Chris Williamson that being easily overwhelmed is a wiring pattern, not a character flaw: a fast, loud amygdala with a prefrontal cortex slow to talk it down. Open-ended uncertainty hurts such people badly, because a brain denied closure manufactures the worst version itself, and falling oestrogen lowers dopamine and makes regulation harder still. Her tools are physical, not philosophical: move, to push oxygenated glucose to the frontal lobe, and delay the reply, to let the alarm cool. What she declines to pathologise is the trait that reads a room, and a child, accurately. — Dr Sasha Hamdani, Chris Williamson
