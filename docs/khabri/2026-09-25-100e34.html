Brugman Will Replace the Swollen Drawer Front

The bathroom-cabinet dispute is settled. Yesterday afternoon Brugman’s customer service wrote that it has ordered a new top drawer front for the vanity unit, and will fit the replacement free of charge — once, as a goodwill gesture. The concession is not an admission: the supplier studied the photos and still holds that the swelling and lifting edges come from outside influences, chiefly moisture left standing (condensation, splashes not dried off) or cleaners used undiluted or too heavily, which the guarantee does not cover. Brugman asks that its care guidelines be followed so it does not happen again. When the new front arrives, it will call to agree a delivery appointment. Nothing is needed from us until then — a good result after the claim was first refused as wear and tear on 16 September.

Deep Dive — Investing for a hundred years

On Hidden Forces, strategist Inigo Fraser Jenkins argues that the rules most portfolios are built on come from an unusual era. From 1980 inflation and interest rates fell for four decades, and shares and bonds tended to move in opposite directions — a cushion investors came to treat as permanent. He thinks AI, climate change and geopolitics now open a far wider range of possible futures, so diversification should mean holding up across very different scenarios, including an inflation shock, rather than simply owning assets that have not moved together in the past. For anyone investing across generations, the goal is to keep purchasing power, not to beat a benchmark, and patient stewardship matters more than the exact mix. A long-horizon view for a household about to gain a new generation — one analyst’s view, not advice. — Hidden Forces, “How to Invest for A Hundred Years”, content digest
