Signed With One, Offered By Another

Saturday morning did in two hours what two months of waiting lists had not. At 10:01 Partou wrote that a place had come free, and gave six days to confirm it. At 12:00 CompaNanny’s system acknowledged a digitally signed contract: Baarsjes, Monday and Wednesday, from 3 May 2027. That is the first dated place the household actually holds — and it is being held loosely. Verlofwet 2028 (the coming overhaul of Dutch leave rules)

▸ Put the whole leave year on one timeline now, before the birth, on today’s rules — and test every headline about the Verlofwet against whether it is actually law yet.The consultation closed on 10 August and the cabinet’s stated aim is 2028; the bill has not even reached the Tweede Kamer. It bundles the existing schemes into three pillars “zonder dat daarmee de verlofduur of de hoogte van de uitkering wordt gewijzigd” — so there is nothing to be won by waiting for it. Today’s deadlines are the ones that bind, and each is hard: one week geboorteverlof within four weeks of the birth, up to five weeks aanvullend geboorteverlof within six months, and the nine paid weeks of ouderschapsverlof inside the first year — for a 29 October baby, gone after end-October 2027. Deep Dive — What Fatherhood Actually Rewires

Matt McCusker, on Chris Williamson’s show, describes the change fatherhood makes as less a decision than a rewiring. The self-absorption goes, he says, because a permanent concern for another person has been installed where it used to sit, and it cannot be evicted. He is unsentimental about the price — the sleep, the physical grind — and clear that the returns come small and constant rather than large and occasional. The wider claim is the interesting one: the empathy does not stop at the child, and strangers start registering as somebody’s. He pairs it with a warning about comfort. With no real threat to face, people lower the bar for what counts as a problem, and manufacture one anyway. — Chris Williamson

The last days of week thirty-three, and forty-six to go. By now the baby has most likely settled head-down and will stay that way; the ones who turn later are a minority.
