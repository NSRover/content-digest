Before the Baby, Settle the Insurance Extras

Sixteen days before the due date is the last quiet moment for a decision that would otherwise land in the newborn weeks. Insurers publish next year’s policies and premiums by 12 November, and switching closes on 31 December. The catch: an insurer must accept anyone for the basic policy, but may refuse the aanvullende (supplementary) cover, or attach a waiting period to it — and that is where our coming year sits: extra kraamzorg hours, dentist, physio. So decide now which cover we need; in November, get the new insurer’s written acceptance for the extras, and ask about waiting periods, before cancelling the old policy. The baby, too, need not join either parent’s insurer: the child’s basic policy is free, but the insurer chosen decides which hospitals and paediatricians are fully covered and what extra cover comes with it. Choose it in the same round. — rijksoverheid.nl, verified 12 Oct

De Adviseur

Bezoekersvergunning (free visitor parking permit)

▸ If family will drive in for the kraamvisite, apply now for the gemeente’s free visitor permit — no car of our own needed. Visitors then park on our street at a discount (65% in most areas, West included) and pay per minute: one permit per address, up to 150 hours a quarter, at most five visitors at once, not in garages or blue zones. Each visit is registered in Mijn Parkeren with DigiD. The application may still be a signed paper form by post with an ID copy, so read the current steps and allow postal time. open now; visits from ~29 Oct — amsterdam.nl, verified 13 Oct

Ouder- en Kindteam (the baby’s health check-ups)

▸ After the birth, expect a home visit in the first week for the heel-prick and hearing test, then a call from a youth nurse between day 10 and 14 to book the first hour-long appointment. If nobody has called by day 14, phone the team (number on the site, weekday mornings). Ours is the Oud West & De Baarsjes team, which also runs the consultatiebureau and walk-in hours for under-fours; appointments can be moved in the online Ouderportaal. from the birth, ~29 Oct — oktamsterdam.nl, verified 13 Oct

De Kleine Khabri — Week 37

Thirty-seven weeks and five days, sixteen days to the due date. The baby is full term, likely around three kilos, and spends these weeks adding fat and practising breathing and sucking; most first babies arrive somewhere between 38 and 41 weeks. Three things parents are discussing this week — their views, not advice: Het thuiskomstpakje (the going-home outfit). On r/BabyBumps a mother-to-be found her own mother had already bought it. Most replies say parents should make these small choices themselves; a gift outfit can get its own moment, such as the grandparents’ first visit. Pick something easy on and off. reddit.com/r/BabyBumps Kleren voor de kraamweek (what mum wears after the birth). Recurring picks on r/beyondthebump: button-front pyjamas or cardigans for feeding, soft high or loose waistbands (kind after a caesarean too), nursing tops, patterns that hide stains — and enough to change often. reddit.com/r/beyondthebump Ook aandacht voor de moeder (asking after mum, not just baby). Many new mothers say visitors suddenly only saw the baby. Suggestions: someone who deliberately asks “how are you?”, contact with other new parents, time set aside for herself — a role the partner can take on now. reddit.com/r/beyondthebump
