Diwali alleen met F1-vuurwerk (only sparkler-class fireworks are legal)

▸ Since 1 August 2026, buying, keeping or setting off consumer fireworks of category F2–F4 is banned all year, not just at New Year. F1 stays legal: sparklers (phuljhari), party poppers, small cake fountains. An anaar or a chakri is F2 or higher, so it is illegal even if an Indian shop or website sells it, or relatives bring it from India, Belgium or Germany; parcels and borders are checked. Read the category on the pack before buying. Any F2 left over from an earlier Diwali can be handed in anonymously and without a fine, but only until today. Tell visitors from India before they pack. De Kleine Khabri — Week 37

Thirty-seven weeks and a day, 20 days to the due date. The baby is full-term now and mostly putting on weight, perhaps around three kilos, while lungs and brain keep maturing; there is less room to move, so kicks may feel more like rolls and stretches. Today’s midwife visit and Monday’s doula meeting are the places to ask about signs of labour and when to call. Elders, hedged and for interest only: Minder doen, hulp toelaten (doing less, letting others help). A mother of a nine-week-old wrote that she wanted to do everything “correctly” herself, until, exhausted at her parents’ house, she let the grandparents take the baby and found it went fine. The most-upvoted replies agreed: don’t go looking for problems in advance, and “if it works, it’s good enough”. With family arriving a week before the due date, worth agreeing who holds the baby while who sleeps. reddit.com/r/NewParents Wat weten we over krampjes? (what is known about colic). A parent of a three-month-old asked about colic, meaning long bouts of crying. Top replies say it is mostly a catch-all name for crying that has no other explanation; one commenter who says they are a paediatrician notes it often eases by three or four months. Parents talking, not medical advice: for a lot of crying, ask the consultatiebureau or GP. reddit.com/r/ScienceBasedParenting
