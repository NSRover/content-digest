Deep Dive — Fifteen Seconds, Not Fifty

Vanessa Van Edwards has spent twenty years watching how people actually talk, and tells The Diary Of A CEO that most conversations die on the opening move: “What do you do?” puts both parties on autopilot, and they recite. Her mechanics are unusually concrete. A healthy conversational turn runs fifteen to thirty seconds — under fifteen starves the other person, past forty-five it is a monologue. Competence without warmth reads as suspicion, which is the trap clever people fall into: they correct, they fact-check, and they are trusted less for it. To leave an awkward conversation without wounding anyone, pair a future cue with a compliment — the brain keeps the ending. Her larger claim is that the loneliness everyone keeps measuring is partly a skills problem: connection moves in three steps, from general traits to real concerns to the story someone tells about their own life, and both jumping ahead and never leaving step one leave people feeling unmet. — The Diary Of A CEO

Week thirty-three, and forty-eight days still to run. The baby is somewhere near two kilos now and roughly the length of a celery stalk; from here it is mostly weight and lung rehearsal. The bones are hardening — all except the skull, which stays deliberately soft so it can fold a little on the way out. The eyes open and close, and the pupils react to light.
