Week thirty-two, day three, and fifty-five still to run. Somewhere near 1.9 kilos and forty-three centimetres; the lungs are still the long job, and most of what is added now is weight rather than length. Elders — a thread worth reading while the room is being arranged. Parents who already have a finished babykamer asked what then still needs to stand in the bedroom, given that the baby sleeps beside the bed for the first months. The answers describe a division of labour: nursery by day for naps, changing and dressing, the wieg in the bedroom for nights only. Several put wheels under the commode so it can move with the day. r/NewParents · And the chair — schommelstoel or fauteuil? Deep Dive — The Reactor Gets Smaller

MacroVoices’ energy roundup with Dr Carly Anderson reads the nuclear revival as a manufacturing story rather than a physics one. The bet is on small modular reactors built on a production line instead of a building site, which is what shrinks lead times from years to months; older plants are being restarted and existing ones uprated at the same time. The customer that makes the arithmetic work is new — data centres, willing to sign long-term power contracts for stable supply — alongside military sites and remote mining communities replacing diesel. Underneath sit two enabling pieces: laser enrichment to modernise the fuel chain, and supercritical CO₂ turbines reaching fifty per cent efficiency in a smaller package. Fusion, she thinks, is a mid-2030s proposition. — MacroVoices #548, Dr Carly Anderson
