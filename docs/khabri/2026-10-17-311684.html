Before the Grandparents Land: No Kajal for Baby

The grandparents fly in from Delhi on Thursday, and in many Indian families a newborn gets kajal or surma around the eyes — often brought from India, sometimes against the evil eye. The US regulator, the FDA, warns plainly against it: kohl, kajal and surma often contain high levels of lead, at times more than half the product’s weight, and it knows of children poisoned by them. Lead is especially harmful to children, and babies of mothers who use these products sometimes carry raised blood lead too. “Herbal” or “natural” on the label changes nothing — the FDA calls them illegal colour additives. What to do: talk it through with the visiting family before the birth, leave any kajal or surma they bring unused, skip it yourself through the kraamtijd and breastfeeding, and ask the consultatiebureau if in doubt. — fda.gov, verified 17 Oct

De Kleine Khabri — Week 38

Thirty-eight weeks and two days; twelve days to the due date. The baby is full term and mostly putting on fat now, which helps it keep warm after birth; the lungs are ready and it keeps practising breathing, swallowing and sucking. Most first babies arrive between 38 and 41 weeks. What parents are discussing this week — their views, not advice: Waarom zo flets? (why are baby clothes so pale?) A new mother on r/BabyBumps asked why nearly all baby clothes come in pale pink, mint or beige. Deep Dive — Focus in a Distracted House

Cognitive neuroscientist Dr. Sahar Yousef of UC Berkeley told The Diary Of A CEO that multitasking is a myth: every switch leaves “attentional residue”, and knowledge workers lose over two hours a day bouncing between screens. Apps are built to exploit that wiring — saturated colour, infinite scroll, unpredictable rewards. Her counter-moves: set the phone to grayscale (she cites about seven hours a week of screen time reclaimed), block “focus sprints” in the calendar with a granular task list, and do the most important task first. Real recovery, she says, is at least seven hours of sleep and passive breaks — nature, or doing nothing — while short-video scrolling adds to the fatigue. Timing matters too: some brains peak in the morning, some late, some twice. A useful brief for the sleep-short weeks ahead. — The Diary Of A CEO (YouTube)
