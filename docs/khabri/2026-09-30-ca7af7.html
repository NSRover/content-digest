Is Korea’s Slide a Warning for Wall Street?

South Korea’s Kospi index has lost more than 40%, argues the market channel Bravos Research, as foreign money left and heavy borrowing on AI names such as Samsung and SK Hynix unwound once the Bank of Korea raised rates. Historically, it says, Kospi falls have run six to eight months ahead of S&P 500 downturns — before the 2000 dot-com bust and the 2022 bear market. America now shows similar strains: record margin debt, leveraged speculation and an index heavy in AI stocks, just as the Fed has begun raising rates. The channel’s own counterpoint: US momentum is still strong, and in 1999 prices kept climbing for months after hikes. Why it matters to us: nearly every name in the strip above is a US-listed stock. One analyst’s warning, not a signal to act. — Bravos Research, “The Unthinkable is About to Happen to Stocks”, content digest

Deep Dive — What WeWork’s founder says he learned

On The Diary of a CEO, Adam Neumann — who built WeWork into a $47 billion company before stepping down under public scrutiny — blames the fall on ego and on a drift from mission to money, with growth outpacing his own development. He now argues that childhood hardship and public failure build resilience rather than break you, and that leadership is influence, not control. Two ideas travel well beyond business: a weekly “technology Shabbat”, 24 hours with every device off, to quiet the noise; and that the biggest decision in life is choosing a partner — whose character, like that of friends and investors, shows on your worst days, not your best. He now runs Flow, a rental-housing company built around community. One founder’s reflection. — The Diary of a CEO, “Adam Neumann”, content digest
