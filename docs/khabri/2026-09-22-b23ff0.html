Mijn Blokje Decides Tomorrow at Ten

Tomorrow morning at ten, at Baarsjesweg 311H, the daycare search reaches the one appointment that actually decides something. Anita Bauer runs both that house and Haarlemmermeerstraat; she does not work Mondays, countered the date that was proposed with a Wednesday, and the reply confirming it went out on Saturday. What rests on the hour is the Monday-and-Tuesday place held there from 3 May 2027 — the pair of days every application made this summer has been built around, and the pair that has been hardest to get anywhere else. The place is held rather than granted, and an hour like this is what converts a reservation or releases it. Nothing is asked of the household beforehand: the tour is the whole errand, and the answer comes out of it.

Deep Dive — The one skill a machine cannot take

Rick Rubin, who built Def Jam and then produced everyone from Johnny Cash to Adele, spent an hour arguing that the useful part of creative work is precisely the part that cannot be justified. Market research and audience expectation, on his account, are what flatten a record; the breakthroughs come from choices that looked indefensible at the time. He is equally blunt about identity — the labels people accept for themselves become prisons, and walking away from something that no longer serves creates a vacuum the world tends to fill. On artificial intelligence he is unbothered: it executes well and has no point of view, and the intention behind the work still has to come from a person. — Rick Rubin, The Diary Of A CEO, content digest

De Kleine Khabri — Week 34, dag 5

Five weeks and two days to go. Somewhere around 45 centimetres and 2.3 kilos now, putting on close to 200 grams a week, and from here almost all of it is fat. The lungs are the organ still finishing: they are making surfactant, the film that keeps the air sacs from collapsing between breaths, and that is the single thing deciding how easily a baby born now would breathe. The fingernails have reached the ends of the fingers. Elders, hedged and for interest only: in r/BabyBumps the parents due this autumn are working out how much family visiting to take on in November and December with a six-week-old. The replies are a spread rather than a rule — some went out within the week and had no regrets, others kept it deliberately small and were glad — and the tip that keeps returning is to keep the baby in a sling at a crowded visit, because then it simply is not passed around. reddit.com/r/BabyBumps And in r/beyondthebump, six hundred-odd replies to a question about which pregnancy complaints vanished the moment the baby did. Rib pain, swollen ankles, heartburn and not being able to draw a full breath went within a day for many; back and tailbone pain, by plenty of other accounts, did not. reddit.com/r/beyondthebump
