Correction: 2027 Child-Allowance Cut Is Off

On 6 October this paper reported that from 2027 the kindgebonden budget (income-tested child allowance) would shrink faster above €57,950 of household income. That bill has stalled. On 17 September the Tweede Kamer passed two motions asking the government to start over; a day later the minister asked the Kamer to shelve it, saying the intention is to withdraw it — along with the later 9.95% version. A new proposal is promised in outline before the year ends; one motion asks for a payment to parents at a birth, but that is only a wish so far. For us, 2027 is the first full year with a child: plan on today’s rules, not the steeper cut. If a 2027 advance arrives in December, check in Mijn toeslagen that our expected joint income leaves out the tax-free expat allowance — overpayments are clawed back. — tweedekamer.nl, verified 14 Oct

Deep Dive — Why Life Loses Its Magic

The historian Robert Greene, author of The Laws of Human Nature, argues that modern life feels flat because screens and social media filter the richness out of reality, and adult brains filter the rest. The ego, he says, keeps us in loops of grievance and petty concern. His remedy is not extreme: deliberately step away from passive scrolling, look at everyday nature with fresh attention, and take in the long scale of history and the sheer improbability of being here at all. He also urges listening to the “daemon” — the inner voice that pulls toward what truly excites us, often impatient with what others expect. Wonder, in his telling, is not lost for good: it waits behind habit, and returns when we choose active attention over passive consumption — noticing nature, or taking the long view of history. A fitting thought for a household about to see the world anew through a newborn’s eyes. — Chris Williamson podcast
