Family Coming for the Birth? Visa Clock Runs

Anyone travelling from India for the birth needs a Schengen visa, and the timing is now tight. NetherlandsWorldwide says an application must be in at least 15 days before travel, and processing currently takes 25 calendar days in the high season — up to 45 if extra documents are requested. For arrival around the due date the hard edge is about 14 October; filing by roughly 5 October keeps the full 25 days. The slowest part happens here: the host signs a sponsorship-and-accommodation form (garantstelling), and the gemeente must legalise that signature, so book that appointment now. Visitors also need travel insurance covering at least €30,000 of medical costs, and may stay 90 days at most. For us: Mukesh and Nisha’s applications have been with the embassy since 12 September, so this matters for anyone else hoping to come. — De Adviseur (expat), netherlandsworldwide.nl, verified 28 Sep

Deep Dive — Could Britain be the next Iceland?

The commentator behind maneco64 warns of a “doom loop” between British government bonds and the pound. His case rests on who owns the debt: since 2008, domestic pension funds and insurers have cut their share of gilts from 46% to about 20%, leaving the market leaning on foreign buyers (34%) and the Bank of England (17%). If yields climb, borrowing costs rise across the economy; if the Bank prints money to hold them down, the pound weakens and inflation quickens — each feeding the other. He names a possible trigger in energy: Britain imports more than half its diesel. Unlike Iceland in 2008 the risk is the currency, not the banks, and he watches $1.30 on sterling. His remedy is gold and silver. One commentator’s view, not a forecast. — maneco64, “Could Britain Be the Next Iceland?”, content digest
