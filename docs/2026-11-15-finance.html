China’s Banks Have Never Done This Before
gemini-flash-lite (gemini-2.5-flash-lite)fallback
TLDR

Chinese banks experienced a net decline in new loans for the first time in October, with repayments exceeding new borrowing, indicating a significant reversal in credit expansion. This contraction, coupled with accelerating sales of non-performing loans and a shift towards government bonds, suggests a banking sector prioritizing self-preservation over financing recovery. The situation is exacerbated by a struggling property market and a reliance on exports tied to the global AI boom, which is now showing signs of weakening.

Key points
  • In October, Chinese financial institutions saw a net decline of 100 billion yuan in new loans, marking the first negative lending flow in October since records began in 1998. This indicates that loan repayments surpassed new borrowing.
  • This negative lending trend is not isolated, as it has occurred three times in 2026 alone, and annual loan growth has slowed to a record low of 4.7%. Household borrowing has also significantly decreased.
  • Chinese banks are actively selling off non-performing loans and issuing asset-backed securities, while simultaneously purchasing large quantities of government bonds. These actions are interpreted as defensive measures to protect the banking system from a crisis.
  • The crisis is characterized by a lack of trust between banks and borrowers, and a retreat of money from productive economic activities, extending beyond the well-known issues in China's property market.
  • Government stimulus measures, including a significant program in September 2024, have failed to restore confidence or revive credit, instead contributing to an artificial intelligence stock bubble that is now collapsing.
  • China's economy has become increasingly dependent on exports linked to the global AI construction boom. A slowdown in this boom could worsen the existing credit downturn.
  • The fundamental assumptions underpinning China's economic model, such as rising property values and reliable borrower income, have broken down, leading to a situation where credit is not generating broad economic recovery but is instead concentrated in state-directed channels.

TLDR

Contrary to mainstream economic claims that rising interest rates and bond yields hurt gold, historical data from periods like the 1970s shows gold soaring alongside double-digit Treasury yields. With today's unprecedented US debt levels, the Federal Reserve faces an impossible choice between permitting rising interest rates that trigger a severe credit crisis or continually printing money, both of which ultimately favor physical gold ownership.

Key points
  • Mainstream analysts claim rising bond yields create headwinds for gold, but historical charts show gold rose from $35 to over $800 during the 1970s while 10-year Treasury yields climbed significantly.
  • High interest rates only suppress gold prices when rates reach extreme levels that drain market liquidity, such as Paul Volcker pushing the discount rate above 20% in 1980 when US debt-to-GDP was just 32%.
  • Because current US debt-to-GDP is around 125% and annual interest expenses have reached $1.3 trillion, the Fed cannot repeat Volcker-era rate hikes to control inflation without triggering a massive debt crisis.
  • Owning physical gold allows an individual to operate as their own central bank, completely offsetting counterparty risk and protecting wealth against systemic monetary debasement.
  • In both potential future economic scenarios—a hyper-inflationary credit expansion by central banks or a severe deflationary credit contraction—physical gold serves as a vital safeguard.
