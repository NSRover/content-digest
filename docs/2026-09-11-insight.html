Vanessa Van Edwards: The Weird Trick That Makes People Like You

Guest

Vanessa Van Edwards is a behavioral researcher and author who has spent 20 years studying the patterns of master conversationalists. She is the author of the books *Cues*, *Captivate*, and her latest work, *Conversation*.

TLDR

Society is facing a connection crisis and a loneliness epidemic, driven by a decline in face-to-face communication skills and an overreliance on digital technology. To build genuine human connection, individuals must move past socially scripted, autopilot questions and learn to navigate three distinct psychological levels of interaction. By mastering conversational turn-taking, recognizing behavioral archetypes, and utilizing non-verbal cues of warmth and competence, anyone can transform awkward small talk into meaningful, frictionless relationships.

Key points
  • Human connection progresses through three distinct psychological levels, starting with Level 1 (General Traits), moving to Level 2 (Personal Concerns), and ending with Level 3 (Self-Narrative). Skipping levels too quickly can cause "vulnerability regret" and make interactions feel invasive, while staying stuck in scripted Level 1 questions like "What do you do?" triggers social autopilot and kills connection.
  • A typical, healthy conversational turn should last between 15 and 30 seconds, with 45 seconds being the absolute maximum before a turn devolves into a monologue. Speaking for less than 15 seconds creates conversational scarcity, while talking too much often stems from a lack of curiosity and can turn a person into a conversational narcissist.
  • Competence without warmth triggers suspicion and distrust in others, a common trap for highly intelligent people who prioritize logic, fact-checking, and truth over emotional connection. To build "rizz" and charisma, individuals must balance competence with warmth by broadcasting clear signals of liking, which helps bridge the "liking gap."
  • People possess one of five fluid conversational and behavioral archetypes that dictate how they communicate and view their lives: the Ambitious Hero, the Contented Pragmatist, the Selfless Helper, the Creative Disruptor, and the Unlucky Struggler. Understanding these archetypes allows individuals to tailor their communication style to match their partner's psychological needs.
  • A person's self-narrative (Level 3) is the story they tell themselves about their life, falling into either a "redemption narrative" (believing hardships can be overcome with resilience) or a "contamination narrative" (feeling like a victim of circumstance). These narratives are not fixed and can be rewritten through supportive, safe conversations and by cultivating an internal locus of control.
  • Non-verbal body language and vocal mechanics heavily influence how warmth and competence are perceived. Charismatic individuals claim physical space, speak at their "maximum resonance point" (the lowest end of their natural vocal range on an outbreath), maintain 60% to 70% eye contact, and utilize active listening cues like the triple nod, head tilt, and lower lid flex.
  • To gracefully exit an awkward conversation without causing offense, speakers should use a "graceful exit" formula that combines a future cue (shifting the brain's focus away from the present) with a compliment or an action. This physical and verbal transition leverages the "peak-end effect," ensuring the interaction ends on a positive, charismatic note.
