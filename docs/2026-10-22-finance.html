Home Sales Just Fell Below 2008 Levels… And It's Getting Worse
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

U.S. pending home sales dropped 7.3% in September to a record low index level of 66, falling below the lowest points recorded during the 2008 financial crisis and the 2020 pandemic shutdowns. This housing downturn is primarily driven by widespread job anxiety and deteriorating consumer confidence in future labor income, rather than mortgage rates alone. Weakness across new starts, building permits, homebuilder sentiment, and downward revisions in employment and retail sales point toward deepening economic recessionary pressures.

Key points
  • The NAR pending home sales index fell 7.3% in September to 66—the lowest level since tracking began in 2001—and unadjusted signed contracts dropped nearly 13% year-over-year, signaling even lower completed sales in the coming months.
  • While real estate associations attribute low transaction volumes to high mortgage rates, historical periods of declining rates failed to revive sales, proving that high borrowing costs are only a secondary factor compared to deep-seated consumer insecurity over employment stability.
  • Housing construction and builder metrics show widespread weakness: September new housing starts fell 5.2% to an annual rate of 1.22 million, private building permits hit their lowest level since early 2023, and the NAHB/Wells Fargo housing market index dropped to 29, a 14-year low.
  • Builders are attempting to clear inventory by offering price reductions (43% of developers) and sales incentives (about two-thirds), yet prospective buyer traffic has collapsed back toward early-pandemic levels even as home supply rose to 4.9 months.
  • Labor market data—such as falling JOLTS job openings, stagnant hiring rates, and reduced voluntary resignations—aligns with major downward revisions in official payroll and retail sales statistics, demonstrating that consumer protection mode is impacting real economic activity.
