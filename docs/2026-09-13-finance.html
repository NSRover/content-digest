The Bubble's Final Phase Has Begun.
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Big tech companies have committed to nearly $3 trillion in AI-related purchases and leases, creating a massive debt burden that is beginning to stress the credit market. This AI boom relies on a fragile financing loop where hyperscalers borrow heavily to build infrastructure for cash-burning AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic, which themselves depend on continuous influxes of investor capital. If liquidity tightens or funding rounds stall, the credit market's rising default swap rates suggest a broader stock market correction could follow.

Key points
  • Big tech companies like Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, and Oracle have committed to nearly $3 trillion in AI-related purchases and leases, a figure representing about 10% of US GDP and dwarfing the debt levels seen during the dot-com bubble and the 2008 subprime mortgage crisis.
  • While the stock market remains at all-time highs, the credit market is showing signs of stress, evidenced by Oracle's credit default swap rates surging to their highest levels since 2009 alongside rising credit risk for other major AI players.
  • Credit investors are historically more sensitive to risk than stock investors because their upside is limited to interest payments while their downside is total loss, meaning credit market distress typically serves as a leading indicator for stock market declines.
  • The entire $2 trillion AI ecosystem relies on a fragile financing loop where debt-funded hyperscalers build data centers to service OpenAI and Anthropic, two cash-burning companies that generate only about $115 billion in combined revenue and rely heavily on external venture capital to pay for this computing power.
  • The sustainability of the AI boom depends heavily on global liquidity and Federal Reserve interest rate policies, as tight liquidity historically forces investors to stop funding cash-burning companies, popping technology bubbles regardless of how transformative the underlying technology eventually becomes.

Michael Oliver: The Bond Market Is Breaking — Gold Knows What’s Coming.
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

A severe global government bond crisis is unfolding across major Western economies and Japan, which central banks will ultimately be forced to address through massive monetary expansion. This looming crisis, combined with accelerating money supply growth, is driving a powerful new leg up in the precious metals bull market. Technical indicators, particularly a 13-year breakout in the gold miners-to-gold ratio, suggest that mining shares are poised to significantly outperform gold.

Key points
  • Historical data refutes the common assumption that rising interest rates are bearish for gold, as demonstrated by major gold rallies during periods of sharp Fed rate hikes in the late 1970s, the mid-2000s, and between 2022 and 2024.
  • The true driver of gold is monetary inflation, measured by money supply growth rather than secondary effects like CPI; the U.S. M2 money supply grew by 8.62% year-over-year as of late July, signaling rapid currency decay.
  • The current sovereign debt crisis is far more severe than past mortgage or dot-com crises, leaving central banks with no choice but to print money to prevent government bond markets from collapsing.
  • A 13-year breakout in the ratio of the Philadelphia Gold and Silver Miners Index (XAU) to gold indicates that mining shares are entering a period of massive outperformance and signaling a major new leg up for gold itself.
  • Recent rapid surges in large-cap mining blue chips like Newmont and Wheaton Precious Metals suggest that institutional asset managers are beginning to rotate capital out of traditional stock and bond portfolios and into liquid mining equities.
  • Technical analysis of the Japanese yen suggests it is shifting into a bull trend against the U.S. dollar, which is poised to trigger a breakdown in the U.S. Dollar Index and further fuel the rally in monetary metals.
