The Midterms Just Exposed How Bad It Really Is…
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Financial markets reacted with total indifference to a sweeping midterm election shift because political changes do not alter the underlying economic system driving stocks or solve root economic distress. High living costs and stagnant real income growth are causing voters globally to repeatedly evict incumbent political parties, even while surface indicators like stock indexes and small positive GDP growth look healthy. True economic recovery requires robust job, credit, and wage growth rather than government spending, temporary rebates, or minor tax adjustments.

Key points
  • Markets ignored the massive shift in congressional power because stock prices depend heavily on liquidity, leverage, corporate buybacks, passive retirement flows, and a few mega-cap global companies rather than the median household's economic reality.
  • Governments lack the ability to force banks to create useful credit, make businesses invest productively, or manufacture good jobs; they can only alter taxes, subsidies, and regulations at the margins.
  • A recurring global "eviction cycle" is taking place across nations—including the U.S., Britain, France, Germany, Japan, and New Zealand—where voters continually vote out incumbent parties that fail to deliver genuine prosperity.
  • Officials continually misdiagnose the affordability crisis as an inflation problem when the underlying issue is a lack of earned job income, weak hiring, and a failure to recover lost purchasing power from earlier supply shocks.
  • The global economy is trapped in a prolonged failure to grow, avoiding a traditional V-shaped crash but repeatedly failing to generate enough wage and productivity growth to repair household balance sheets.
