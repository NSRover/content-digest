TLDR

The global race for critical minerals is intensifying as the era of globalization transitions into a multipolar world dominated by geopolitics and resource security. Tungsten has emerged as a key battleground, with China and North Korea controlling 85% of global production and prompting Western governments, like the UK, to directly invest in local mining projects to secure supply chains. This shift signals a secular bull market for commodities, where real assets are poised to outperform financial equities and bonds.

Key points
  • The UK government's National Wealth Fund recently invested £71 million in Tungsten West, a mining project in Devon, securing a 7% equity stake and the right to buy up to half of the mine's annual production.
  • Tungsten is an exceptionally dense, hard metal with the highest melting point of any pure metal, making it indispensable for cutting tools, electronics, aerospace, and defense applications like armor-piercing projectiles.
  • China and North Korea produce approximately 85% of the world's tungsten, and recent Chinese export curbs have contributed to a tenfold increase in the price of the metal over the last year and a half.
  • The ratio of the Commodity Research Bureau (CRB) index to the S&P 500 shows that despite recent corrections, commodities remain highly undervalued compared to historical peaks in 2008 and 1980, signaling a long-term secular bull market.
  • Former Credit Suisse strategist Zoltan Pozsar predicted in late 2022 that the global economy is shifting from financialization to an era of resource security, where the actions of statesmen and geopolitical alliances like BRICS matter more to inflation and markets than central bank policies.
  • The decline of the traditional petrodollar recycling system, where foreign nations automatically reinvested dollar surpluses back into US Treasuries, is fundamentally altering global bond demand and supporting the rise of commodity values.
