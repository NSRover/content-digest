  • The average interest rate on all US debt has risen from 1.77% in 2020 to 3.49% today, causing the government's annual interest payments to balloon from $523 billion to approximately $1.3 trillion.
  • The Treasury has doubled its buyback operations of older, "off-the-run" long-term securities from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation, which officially aims to improve liquidity in thinly traded areas of the market.
  • Skeptics argue the buybacks are a backdoor attempt to lower long-term yields and reduce future borrowing costs, bypassing the independent Federal Reserve.
  • Unlike quantitative easing by the Federal Reserve, the Treasury cannot print money to fund buybacks; it must finance them through taxes, cash balances, or by issuing short-term Treasury bills, which merely shifts the debt structure rather than reducing it.
