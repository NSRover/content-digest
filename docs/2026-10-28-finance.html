Elon Musk Warns Banks Have STOPPED Lending to AI Companies
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Credit markets are showing severe resistance toward funding AI companies that lack substantial revenue outside of AI data centers, forcing firms to seek higher interest rates, equity financing, or supplier guarantees. This tightening in AI debt access coincides with a broader credit cycle downturn, evidenced by surging Triple C spreads, rising investment-grade spreads, and near-pandemic levels of distressed leveraged loans. As mega-cap firms and massive AI projects consume available financial capacity, weaker neoclouds and traditional non-AI borrowers face an increasingly tight credit crunch.

Key points
  • Debt Markets Are Rejecting Pure AI Speculation: Lenders are no longer willing to fund AI data center projects based purely on future demand projections. Access to debt markets has largely shut down for borrowers that do not possess substantial, independent cash flow from non-AI businesses.
  • Hyperscalers and AI Firms Face Stricter Financing Terms: Major firms are being forced to adapt to credit market resistance. Meta is attempting a massive ~$98 billion equity financing program to offset deteriorating cash flows, while banks nearly backed out of a $63 billion chip debt package for Anthropic until supplier Broadcom agreed to higher interest rates and residual value guarantees to absorb default risk.
  • Neoclouds and Pure-Play AI Borrowers Are Under Distress: Neocloud providers like CoreWeave are seeing their bonds plunge to distressed levels, with credit markets pricing in a greater than 60% probability of default within five years. The broader neocloud equity and debt sectors are dropping as current revenue fails to support their debt-heavy capital structures.
  • Deterioration Is Spreading Across Broader Credit Markets: The credit tightening is not isolated to AI. Triple C corporate spreads have climbed above 1,350 basis points, investment-grade spreads are widening, publicly traded private credit firms are reaching new lows, and distressed leveraged loans trading below 80 cents on the dollar have climbed to nearly $140 billion—approaching May 2020 highs.
  • Technology Sector Dominates Leveraged Loan Distress: Technology accounts for roughly 39% ($54 billion) of all distressed leveraged loans. Because many of these private-equity-backed firms rely on floating-rate debt and expected cooperative markets to refinance near-term maturities, selective lending poses an immediate threat to their viability.
  • Financial System Capacity Threatens the Macro Economy: Massive capital demands from AI expansion threaten to crowd out ordinary non-AI borrowers, including retailers, commercial real estate, and manufacturers. The mismatch between AI's massive future capital requirements and the financial system's shrinking risk tolerance signals a deepening macro credit cycle downturn.
