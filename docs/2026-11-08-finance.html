China’s “Government-Backed Companies Are Missing Payments. How Bad Is China’s Debt?

TLDR

China’s real estate and credit crisis has entered a major new escalation as Zhuhai Huafa Group, a massive state-owned conglomerate, missed payments on trust products and requested loan extensions. This technical default exposes deep-seated bad debt within China's financial sector and shows that implied government backing no longer guarantees timely payment. Consequently, risk-averse Chinese banks are sharply reducing private lending and fleeing into safe-haven government bonds, driving sovereign yields to record lows. This systemic banking crisis is restricting the massive credit required to fund China's domestic technology and AI ambitions, directly contributing to a sharp route in Chinese equities.

Key points
  • Zhuhai Huafa Group, a major Guangdong local government-owned developer with roughly 62 billion USD equivalent in interest-bearing liabilities, missed trust payments and sought bank loan extensions. This failure shows China's property crisis expanding beyond private developers into state-owned entities previously viewed as safe.
  • Despite reassuring regulatory statistics on loan performance, Chinese banks are acting defensively by curtailing new private lending, avoiding risky borrowers, and heavily purchasing government bonds. This behavior has pushed Chinese sovereign yields to record lows, reflecting classic depression economics and a silent banking crisis.
  • The route in Chinese equities—including severe declines in the CSI 300 and Star 50 indices—is heavily driven by the realization that Chinese banks lack the balance sheet capacity to fund the capital-intensive domestic AI and technology supply chain alongside real estate bailouts.
  • Beijing’s emergency interventions, including central bank funding for share buybacks and informal moral suasion instructing institutions not to sell equities, demonstrate that officials are attempting to interrupt an active market liquidation rather than managing a healthy financial recovery.
  • Rescuing state-backed firms like Huafa through loan extensions and debt restructurings simply transfers underlying economic losses across the public sector, tying up capital and ensuring that broader credit availability will remain restricted.
