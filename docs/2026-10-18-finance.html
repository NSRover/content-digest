The US Debt Crisis Is No Longer Coming — It's Already Here!
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

The US Treasury confirmed that the fiscal year 2026 gross interest on the national debt reached $1.36 trillion, while the federal deficit rose to $1.992 trillion. Combined with major mandatory spending programs, gross interest payments now consume over 100% of federal tax receipts, signaling an accelerating debt spiral. As foreign buyers grow increasingly reluctant to absorb massive amounts of US debt, the Federal Reserve will likely be forced to step in with trillions in quantitative easing, leading to further currency debasement.

Key points
  • Gross interest on US federal debt totaled $1.36 trillion for FY2026, marking a 12% increase from the prior year and surpassing defense spending by roughly 40% to 50%.
  • Under a "true interest expense" framework—which combines gross interest with spending on Medicare, Medicaid, general health, and Veterans Affairs—mandatory obligations currently equal 105% of total tax receipts, meaning all other government functions must be financed with borrowed money.
  • The US financial system is facing structural liquidity issues in part because bond primary dealers have not grown proportionally to handle the massive volume of new treasury issuances, widening bid-ask spreads and worsening market mechanics in a high-yield environment.
  • Projections for FY2027 estimate the federal deficit rising to $2.25 trillion in a standard base case scenario, or up to $2.5 trillion if the economy enters a recession or bond yields rise sharply.
  • Foreign nations are increasingly diversifying their reserves away from US Treasuries and into physical gold due to weaponized sanctions and asset-freezing policies, making it unlikely that international buyers will rescue the US treasury market in a future downturn.
