A leaked S-1 filing from Anthropic reveals staggering projected expenses of $518 billion over the next few years against just $4.6 billion in revenue for 2025. This massive imbalance highlights that the AI boom is a debt-driven bubble structurally identical to the dot-com era, relying on complex credit guarantees and mispriced debt. A sooner-rather-than-later collapse of this bubble is preferable to prevent catastrophic, widespread damage to the credit system and the broader economy.

Key points
  • Anthropic's leaked S-1 prospectus shows a massive financial mismatch, with 2025 revenue at $4.6 billion alongside $8 billion in operating losses, $42 billion in total losses when accounting for capital expenditures, and a staggering $518 billion in planned spending over the next few years.
  • Despite these deep losses, Anthropic is seeking an IPO valuation of $2 trillion, up from its previous private valuation of $950 billion, demonstrating that the AI market is priced for "ultra-perfection" where absolutely nothing can go wrong.
  • The AI boom mirrors the dot-com bubble's debt-driven structure; while giants like Nvidia and Broadcom appear highly profitable today, they act like dot-com era equipment suppliers (such as Nortel and Lucent) by backing the debt of unprofitable startups to artificially fuel their own sales.
  • Wall Street is using complex financial engineering, including Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) and private credit, to fund AI capital expenditures. Tech giants like Broadcom provide "residual value support" (guarantees) on rapidly depreciating chip collateral to secure artificially low interest rates for startups.
  • This interconnected web of guarantees and distributed debt means a failure in parabolic revenue growth will trigger a severe repricing of debt across the entire credit market, which is already tightening and becoming increasingly inelastic.

TLDR

The South Korean stock market (Kospi Index) has historically served as an early warning sign for the US stock market, with its declines preceding S&P 500 downturns by several months. This relationship is attributed to South Korea's central bank tending to hike interest rates before the Federal Reserve, impacting highly leveraged markets. The US market currently exhibits similar vulnerabilities to South Korea's prior crashes, including high margin debt and concentration in AI-related stocks, suggesting a potential future downturn as the Federal Reserve begins to raise interest rates.

Key points
  • The South Korean Kospi Index experienced a significant decline, losing over 40% of its value, driven by an exodus of foreign institutional capital and an unwind of excessive margin debt, particularly in concentrated AI-related stocks like Samsung and SK Hynix.
  • Historically, declines in the Kospi Index have preceded downturns in the S&P 500 by approximately six to eight months, as seen before the dot-com bubble burst in 2000 and the 2022 bear market.
  • The trigger for the South Korean market's collapse was the Bank of Korea's decision to hike interest rates, which made borrowed money (margin debt) more expensive and forced a deleveraging event through margin calls.
  • The US stock market currently shares similar vulnerabilities, including record-high margin debt (nearly 50 times larger than South Korea's in a market only 10 times bigger), high speculation in leveraged products, and significant concentration in AI-related companies within the S&P 500.
  • The Federal Reserve has recently begun raising interest rates, following the Bank of Korea's earlier hikes, which historically signals a potential shift towards tighter financial conditions for the US market.
  • While historical patterns suggest a potential downturn, market momentum is also a crucial factor; in 1999, the market continued to rise for months after Fed rate hikes due to strong momentum, whereas in 2022, momentum was already weakening.
  • Despite recent Fed rate hikes, current market momentum in the US remains strong, with prices above key moving averages, indicating that a market breakdown has not yet occurred.
