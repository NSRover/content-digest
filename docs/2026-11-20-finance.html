Home Sales Just Crashed 18%. Everyone Is Missing the Real Warning.
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

US pending home sales dropped 18% over a six-month period to a record low, driven by a vanishing pool of buyers rather than the forced foreclosures seen in 2008. Weakening consumer confidence in future employment and incomes is keeping buyers away, resulting in sellers outnumbering buyers by over 70%. If housing activity shifts into falling home prices, the loss of the wealth effect could amplify broader economic fragility, potentially forcing the Federal Reserve to pivot toward rate cuts.

Key points
  • Pending home sales fell 4.7% in October and nearly 18% over six months, reaching the lowest level since tracking began in 2001, with contract activity dropping across all US regions.
  • Unlike the 2008 financial crisis, which was triggered by subprime defaults and forced bank foreclosures, the current real estate crash is driven by an unprecedented decline in buyer demand while existing owners hold low fixed-rate mortgages.
  • Redfin data shows sellers outnumber buyers by 70.5%, with active buyer numbers reaching their lowest levels since 2013, shifting negotiating power heavily toward buyers.
  • Single-family housing starts remain weak, and single-family building permits dropped to a nearly four-year low, indicating that home builders are losing confidence in future demand.
  • A potential decline in home prices threatens the direct "wealth effect" that has sustained consumer spending, creating a feedback loop of cautious borrowing, lower consumer activity, and further economic weakness.
  • While the Federal Reserve maintains a hawkish stance on inflation, bond and swap markets are pricing in long-term economic deceleration that could force policy rate cuts.

Precious and industrial metals face temporary headwinds from high interest rates, a strong US dollar, and short-term speculative unwinds, while high distillate prices and restricted global refining capacity remain persistent inflationary factors.

Key points
  • Combined net speculative positions across 25 major commodity futures fell 77% over two months to early-2026 lows, while the commodity sector gained roughly 25% year-to-date up to November 2026, primarily driven by energy.
  • Ole Hansen attributes long-term support for the commodity supercycle to structural drivers, including EV adoption, power grid electrification, supply tightness, geopolitical de-globalization, and a move toward "just in case" inventory management.
  • Restricted global refining capacity from missing Middle East and Russian capacity keeps diesel prompt prices and crack spreads high, sustaining global freight costs and inflation independently of crude oil flat price declines.
  • Precious metals are undergoing a structural correction within a multi-year bull trend following an 80% gain in 2025; long-term factors like central bank interest rate policies and rising US front-end yield issuance continue to support investor demand for hard assets.
  • Copper's drop below its 200-day moving average stems from a speculative position wash-out linked to short-term tech fears rather than broken long-term fundamentals, with exchange-monitored copper stockpiles exceeding 1 million tons.
