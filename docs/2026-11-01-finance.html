Invest....(but where?). Everything is falling: Gold, Nifty, AI Stocks
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Navigating volatile markets requires structural portfolio planning rather than attempting to time cycles or panic-selling during pullbacks. Investors should avoid holding excessive cash due to inflation risks from money printing, focusing instead on diversifying across geographies, currencies, and non-correlated asset classes. By understanding support levels and distinguishing between short-term price drops and fundamentally broken businesses, investors can confidently buy quality assets on dips.

Key points
  • Structural portfolio management relies on broad diversification across asset classes, geographies, and currencies—such as combining domestic equities, foreign stocks, real estate, and precious metals—to hedge against local currency devaluation and economic volatility.
  • Investors must avoid chasing assets during growth peaks and instead focus on accumulating fundamentally strong assets during consolidation phases or when prices touch established support levels.
  • A critical investing distinction must be made between a "broken price" (a temporary price drop caused by short-term factors like elevated capital expenditure) and a "broken business" (a company facing structural obsolescence due to technological displacement).
  • Long-term market stagnations can stem from either structural collapses or cyclical downturns; recognizing these trends allows investors to manage risk by reducing exposure to stagnant markets and reallocating capital to higher-growth opportunities.
  • A disciplined framework involves allocating roughly two-thirds of investable funds into growth equities and one-third into stable assets like gold or bonds, while raising cash buffers near all-time high valuations to fund future dip-buying opportunities.

America's Debt Time Bomb Is Ticking Faster Than Ever Before.

TLDR

The US national debt is growing at an accelerating rate of $7.6 billion per day, nearing $41 trillion, driven by a $2 trillion annual budget deficit and a secular bear market in bonds. The Fed will likely be forced to step in with liquidity creation and currency expansion, particularly if a stock market crash or recession further shrinks tax revenues and expands the deficit. Physical gold and silver, held directly outside the financial and digital ecosystem, serve as crucial protection against monetary debasement, market irrationality, and counterparty risks.

Key points
  • The US national debt is expanding exponentially, taking just 70 days to grow from $40 trillion to $40.58 trillion, compared to the 205 years it took to reach its first $1 trillion.
  • The US is running an annual budget deficit of roughly $2 trillion (around 6% of GDP) during a non-recessionary period, with about $10 trillion in debt needing to be refinanced over the next 12 months at higher interest rates.
  • The bond market has entered a long-term bear market of rising yields, ending a 40-year bull market; a stock market crash in big tech or AI could trigger a recession that pushes budget deficits to 8% or 9%.
  • An economic slowing or recession will force the Federal Reserve to intervene with new liquidity injections, regardless of what new acronym they use to disguise Quantitative Easing (QE).
  • Government fiscal and monetary policies are out of control, evidenced by US M2 money supply growing by nearly 6% over the past year, while similar debt crises unfold in the UK where national debt has passed £3 trillion.
  • Paper prices of precious metals are manipulated through derivatives and futures markets, but holding physical gold and silver avoids leverage, system exposure, and digital risks such as exchange hacks.
