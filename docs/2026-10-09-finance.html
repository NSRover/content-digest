Peter Carlin: The Global Energy Crisis Is Going From Bad to Worse.
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

The global energy crisis is worsening due to severe diesel shortages, soaring oil tanker shipping rates, and refinery shutdowns, which are further strained by escalating geopolitical tensions. This energy disruption is acting as a major catalyst for a sovereign debt crisis, putting immense pressure on global bond markets as governments continue to issue debt. Despite a strong US dollar against weaker currencies, gold and silver remain highly resilient, positioned for a massive future surge once the capital-draining AI bubble inevitably bursts.

Key points
  • The global diesel market is facing severe supply constraints as China and Russia restrict exports, India's product exports drop by 50%, and US refineries prepare for seasonal maintenance. To cope, Western nations are resorting to emergency measures, including changing diesel specifications to allow more air pollution and pressuring the EU to release its fuel reserves.
  • Oil tanker shipping rates have skyrocketed to extreme levels, with Time Charter Equivalent (TCE) rates for VLCC tankers in the Gulf of Oman jumping from $125,000 to $1.2 million per day. This massive profit potential in the Middle East has drawn ships away from other regions, causing shipping costs to explode globally, including routes from West Africa, Guyana, and the Black Sea.
  • The United Kingdom's domestic refining capacity has collapsed from 18 refineries in the 1980s to just four active facilities today. With major shutdowns for maintenance and recent refinery bankruptcies, the UK has become highly vulnerable, relying on the US for one-third of its diesel imports.
  • While geopolitical escalations initially drive financial institutions to seek liquidity in the US dollar, the dollar is only strong against declining currencies like the euro, yen, and sterling. Against the Chinese yuan, the dollar has weakened despite a massive interest rate differential, signaling underlying structural weakness in the US currency system.
  • Gold continues to perform exceptionally well in a high-interest-rate environment, defying traditional economic expectations. This resilience is heavily supported by physical cash accumulation from central banks, particularly China, which purchased 730,000 ounces of gold in September.
  • The massive influx of global capital into the artificial intelligence (AI) sector has starved the real economy of essential capital expenditures, leading to underinvestment in oil, gas, and mining infrastructure. A projected collapse or correction in this AI "bubble" is expected to trigger a broader stock market sell-off, ultimately initiating an unprecedented panic-driven rush into gold and silver.
