Guest

Robert Pape is a political scientist, professor at the University of Chicago, and author. He specializes in the study of political violence and is the director of the University of Chicago's Center for Security and Threat Projects.

TLDR

The United States is experiencing an unprecedented era of "violent populism," driven by demographic shifts and wealth concentration, leading to tens of millions of Americans on both the left and right supporting political violence. This widespread support, amplified by social media and a lack of political leadership to condemn it, makes upcoming elections, particularly the midterms and the potential transfer of power in 2027, the most dangerous in recent history. Ordinary people are becoming radicalized due to fears of losing everything if the opposing party wins, creating a "negative sum society" where both sides believe they are falling behind.

Key points
  • The greatest threat to the United States is not external but internal, stemming from tens of millions of Americans across the political spectrum who support political violence. This era, termed "violent populism," is distinct from previous periods of unrest due to its widespread, bipartisan nature.
  • Support for political violence is alarmingly high, with significant percentages of both Democrats and Republicans expressing willingness to use force to prevent the opposing party's political outcomes, including extreme measures like assassination. This widespread support creates a dangerous environment for upcoming elections.
  • The stereotype of a political rebel has shifted from young, unemployed individuals to highly educated, middle-class Americans with stable lives. These individuals are increasingly engaging in or supporting violence because they fear losing their livelihoods and way of life if the opposing political party gains power.
  • Political violence is driven by core societal changes, primarily demographic shifts (the transition to a white-minority democracy) and increasing wealth concentration. These factors create a "negative sum society" where many feel they are falling behind, fueling anxiety and a belief that the opposing side will actively harm them.
  • Political leaders on both sides are failing to adequately restrain their own supporters from engaging in or advocating for political violence. This lack of condemnation normalizes extreme actions and perpetuates a cycle of escalating hostility, making a "legitimacy shock" where election results are not accepted a significant risk.
  • Social media plays a crucial role in radicalization by amplifying provocative comments and creating a "chain reaction" that can escalate support for violence into actual violent acts. The absence of a strong "silent majority" voice against political violence further exacerbates this issue.
  • While foreign manipulation can amplify existing social divisions, it is not the primary cause of the current unrest. The fundamental drivers are internal social changes and the resulting anxieties, which will persist even if specific political figures or media personalities are removed from the public sphere.
