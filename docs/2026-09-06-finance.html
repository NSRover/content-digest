Convenience Stores Just Issued a Warning About the American Consumer
gemini-flash (gemini-2.5-flash)fallback
TLDR

Despite headlines suggesting a resilient American consumer, strong evidence indicates a shift in behavior due to an energy shock and weakening economic conditions. Consumers are paying more for essentials like fuel but reducing discretionary spending, leading to demand destruction across various sectors. This trend is reflected in declining sales volumes, increased promotions, and businesses postponing long-term commitments like construction, signaling a broader economic slowdown rather than sustained inflation.

Key points
  • Convenience stores like Circle K are reporting record fuel revenue due to higher prices, but actual fuel volumes are declining, and customers are making fewer store visits and becoming more selective about discretionary purchases like snacks and drinks. This illustrates "demand destruction" where higher energy costs force consumers to cut back elsewhere.
  • The labor market is showing signs of weakness, with private payroll data slowing and job openings declining. Companies like Uber are eliminating jobs, and a decrease in voluntary quits suggests reduced worker confidence in finding new employment, indicating a shift towards "no hire and some fire."
  • Discretionary spending is significantly impacted, as evidenced by collapsing resale ticket prices for events like the US Open, increased promotions at grocery stores, and higher-income households trading down to discount retailers like Dollar General.
  • Construction spending, both residential and non-residential, is declining, which is a strong indicator of businesses and households losing confidence in future demand and income, as these projects require long-term commitments. This weakness in construction challenges the idea that lower interest rates automatically stimulate growth.
  • The overall picture suggests that while nominal consumer spending might appear stable due to higher prices, the underlying real activity is contracting. This widespread behavioral change, from convenience stores to luxury home sales, indicates a fragile economy where an energy shock leads to impoverishment and demand destruction, not sustained inflation.

TLDR

The United States is facing an escalating fiscal crisis as its national debt surpasses $40 trillion, forcing the government to borrow $1 trillion every 70 days. Rising long-term bond yields, driven by "bond vigilantes" demanding higher term premiums, are squeezing the federal budget by pushing interest payments to 20% of tax revenues. To prevent a sovereign debt crisis, the Federal Reserve is projected to intervene through financial repression—printing money to buy government bonds and artificially capping yields—which historically triggers severe inflation and devalues currency.

Key points
  • The US government's borrowing pace has accelerated parabolically, now requiring $1 trillion in new debt every 70 days compared to every two years in the 2000s. With the debt ceiling at $41.1 trillion and no credible spending constraints, the debt-to-GDP ratio has climbed above 120%.
  • Institutional investors, known as bond vigilantes, are driving up long-term interest rates by demanding a higher term premium to compensate for risks like inflation and fiscal recklessness. This has pushed the cost of 10-year and 30-year government borrowing to their highest levels in decades.
  • Interest payments on the national debt now consume 20% of total US tax revenues, a level not seen since the late 1970s and early 1980s when interest rates were twice as high. If yields continue to rise, interest payments could eventually consume up to 50% of the government's budget.
  • While policymakers argue the US can grow its way out of debt, demographic trends suggest otherwise. A declining birth rate since 1990 has led to a shrinking labor force participation rate, which historically correlates with a rising debt-to-GDP ratio and makes economic outperformance unlikely.
  • According to the IMF's "R minus G" rule, a sovereign debt crisis becomes inevitable when the average interest rate on government debt exceeds nominal GDP growth. Projections show these two metrics crossing by 2030, or as early as late 2027 if bond yields rise by just one additional percentage point.
  • To prevent a budget collapse, the Federal Reserve is expected to utilize its forgotten third mandate—moderating long-term interest rates—by printing money to buy government bonds. This policy of financial repression mirrors the Fed's actions in 1942, which successfully lowered the debt-to-GDP ratio but caused inflation to spike to 20% by 1947, halving the purchasing power of the US dollar.
