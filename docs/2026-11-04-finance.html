Hong Kong and London Are Preparing Gold’s Big Comeback – Eric Yeung.
gemini-3-5-flash (gemini-3.5-flash)
TLDR

Financial institutions in Hong Kong and London are moving toward adopting physical gold—potentially via blockchain tokenization—as high-quality liquid asset (HQLA) collateral to back fiat currencies. This shift could phase out or constrain the highly leveraged LBMA paper market system by replacing untracked rehypothecation with transparent, real-time tracking on a blockchain. Shrinking paper leverage and creating institutional demand for tokenized gold collateral is expected to provide strong long-term price support for physical precious metals.

Key points
  • Sovereign states and institutions are preparing for physical gold to return as a primary provider of global fiat liquidity, replacing or supplementing US Treasury bonds as reserve collateral.
  • Following the London Metal Exchange's (LME) adoption of base metals as collateral, the newly established over-the-counter gold hub in Hong Kong and the Bank of England/LBMA are evaluating tokenized gold on blockchain ledgers for liquidity provision.
  • Current paper-to-physical ratios at the LBMA—estimated at up to 400-to-1 for gold and 1,000-to-1 for silver—rely on unrestricted rehypothecation. Tokenizing physical gold on a transparent ledger allows institutions to establish controlled, real-time tracked repo limits that prevent unlimited paper leverage.
  • China and major Western institutions may be coordinating during this accumulation phase, using short paper positions on COMEX to allow central banks to buy physical bullion outside the US through over-the-counter markets.
  • Judy Shelton's proposal for 50-year gold-backed US Treasury bonds faces skepticism, as a 50-year redemption period fails to offer the immediate physical backing that modern central banks and foreign hubs demand.
  • Blockchain-based gold tokenization offers a middle ground between the deflationary constraints of a strict 1-to-1 metal standard and the hyperinflationary risk of unconstrained paper leverage, providing controlled elasticity for fiat system liquidity.
