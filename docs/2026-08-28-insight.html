Guest

Kanika Batra is an Australian content creator, author, and former pageant competitor. Diagnosed with antisocial personality disorder (ASPD) and narcissistic personality disorder (NPD), she uses her platform to share her experiences as a diagnosed sociopath and to teach women strategies to navigate manipulative relationships.

TLDR

In this interview, Kanika Batra shares her internal experience living with antisocial personality disorder (ASPD) and narcissistic personality disorder (NPD). She discusses her lack of empathy, her struggle with chronic boredom, and her history of seeking calculated revenge. Batra also details how she uses her understanding of manipulation to advise women in the dating world, her recent conversion to Orthodox Christianity as a framework for pro-social behavior, and how she maintains a stable marriage despite her diagnosis.

Key points
  • The Internal Reality of ASPD and NPD: Batra describes her primary psychopathy (factor one) as a complete lack of anxiety, fear, and guilt, noting that her amygdala does not register emotional responses on brain scans. While she can cognitively understand what others feel, she must consciously mimic emotional responses like crying. She experiences a flat, non-sad depression and chronic boredom, with fleeting positive emotions that last only minutes, while negative emotions like rage and contempt persist much longer.
  • A History of Calculated Revenge and Sadism: Batra admits to experiencing sadism, deriving a sense of power and adrenaline from watching others suffer or beg for emotional closure. She holds long-term grudges and has carried out elaborate, long-term revenge schemes against online harassers and personal rivals, using tactics ranging from social sabotage to sending disturbing packages.
  • Dating Advice and the "Science of Manipulation": Batra advocates for teaching women manipulative dating tactics as a defense mechanism against toxic male behaviors. Her advice includes withholding sex early on, using "utility tests" to gauge a partner's genuine interest, and maintaining strategic emotional distance (such as disabling read receipts) to avoid becoming emotionally dependent too quickly.
  • The Dynamics of a Sociopathic Marriage: Despite her tendency to develop intense contempt for romantic partners after about three months, Batra has maintained a stable marriage. She attributes this to her husband's calm, loyal nature, their shared conservative political beliefs, and their efforts to avoid domestic monotony by traveling and pursuing unconventional shared interests.
  • Conversion to Orthodox Christianity: Seeking structure, discipline, and a path toward redemption, Batra recently converted to Orthodox Christianity. She is drawn to the church's ancient traditions, its strict hierarchies, and its theological view of the church as a "hospital" for sinners rather than a courtroom, using her faith and the guidance of a spiritual father to consciously build pro-social habits.

Guest

Ed Zitron is a tech critic, PR executive, and CEO of the primary research firm EZPR. He is the host of the 'Better Offline' podcast, author of the newsletter 'Where's Your Ed At', and author of the book *The Hater's Guide To Silicon Valley* (expected Q2 2027).

TLDR

The generative AI industry is a massive, unsustainable bubble driven by deceptive corporate narratives, hidden losses, and artificial demand. Leading AI firms like OpenAI and Anthropic are burning billions of dollars on unprofitable subscription models that heavily subsidize token usage, while tech giants overspend on massive data center infrastructure to inflate their stock prices. This speculative cycle is projected to collapse around 2027, triggering a severe tech depression and a broader economic recession due to the stock market's heavy reliance on a few overvalued tech giants.

Key points
  • Generative AI is a financially unsustainable con: The leading AI companies operate at horrifying losses, with OpenAI and Anthropic accounting for 70% of all generative AI revenues while relying on billions in corporate subsidies to survive. Because flat-rate subscription fees do not cover the actual cost of the massive "token" usage by consumers and enterprises, these companies heavily subsidize user activity at an unsustainable loss.
  • Forced adoption and declining software quality: Rather than experiencing organic consumer demand, generative AI is being pushed non-consensually onto users through aggressive integration into everyday software like Google, Microsoft Word, and Amazon. Despite promises of revolutionary productivity, the widespread use of Large Language Models (LLMs) for coding is actually making software buggier and causing increased instability across major platforms.
  • A massive mismatch in data center capacity: Tech companies are spending trillions on capital expenditures to build massive, power-hungry data centers to speculate on generative AI demand. However, there is a massive chasm between the planned 190-gigawatt capacity—which requires trillions in annual demand to break even—and the actual market demand, which is currently driven almost entirely by tech giants funding their own partners.
  • The myth of rapid, continuous AI improvement: AI models have hit a plateau, only showing improvement on rigged benchmarks they were specifically trained to pass rather than gaining genuine general intelligence. True autonomous "agentic AI" remains a marketing myth, as current systems still require extensive human-built guardrails and "harnesses" to mitigate persistent, confident hallucinations.
  • The threat of a looming "tech depression" by 2027: The AI bubble is projected to burst around 2027 when unprofitable startups run out of cash and fail to secure further funding. Because the valuations of tech giants, venture capital portfolios, and the broader stock market are deeply intertwined with AI infrastructure spending, the collapse of OpenAI could trigger a severe economic recession and wipe out retirement portfolios.
  • Deceptive metrics and lack of regulatory oversight: Tech companies use misleading financial metrics like "annualized run rates" to obscure their actual AI revenues and manipulate stock prices. This deception is enabled by a non-functional SEC and a media environment that lacks critical skepticism, allowing tech CEOs to face no consequences for misleading the public.
