History is About to Be Made... (Emergency Update)

TLDR

A critical divergence has emerged between AI infrastructure providers and the hyperscalers funding them, signaling that the AI market may have reached the "peak of inflated expectations" on the Gartner hype cycle. Historically, technological bubbles like the 1840s railway mania and the 2000 dot-com crash peaked when capital investment crossed 7% of GDP and interest rates rose. While the current cheap money environment allows hyperscalers to continue borrowing and spending on AI, a future rise in interest rates above 5.5% could trigger a severe market correction.

Key points
  • A major divergence has appeared between AI infrastructure stocks (like Nvidia, Broadcom, and TSMC) and hyperscalers (like Amazon and Meta), with infrastructure stocks going parabolic while hyperscalers have remained flat due to weak returns on their massive AI investments.
  • Multiple corporations are struggling to realize a clear return on investment for their AI spending, highlighted by Uber burning its annual AI budget in four months with minimal product improvement, and Microsoft restricting internal AI tool usage due to escalating costs.
  • The AI sector has grown to represent 20% of the S&P 500, meaning any potential correction in these dominant stocks will have a much more severe impact on the broader market than in previous eras when they made up only 2% to 4% of the index.
  • Total capital flowing into AI has reached 8% of US GDP, crossing the historical 7% threshold that marked the peak of both the 1840s railway mania in Great Britain and the 2000 dot-com bubble in the United States.
  • Historical bubbles did not pop purely due to high valuation, but rather when central banks raised interest rates, increasing debt-servicing costs and forcing investors to demand immediate returns on investment.
  • Hyperscalers have capitalized on cheap money by raising $244 billion in AI-related debt in 2026 alone, meaning the AI spendout can continue until the Federal Reserve raises interest rates back above the 5.5% threshold.
